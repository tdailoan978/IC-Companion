# Lesson 1 · 中国的节日 · China's Holidays

## Objectives
- After this lesson you can name the major traditional Chinese holidays, give their lunar-calendar dates with 正月 and 初, and say which foods go with each one.
- After this lesson you can describe New Year customs such as the upside-down 福, the fish left over at dinner, red envelopes and New Year calls.
- After this lesson you can raise a cup and wish people success, progress, good health and a smooth year, including toasting with tea instead of alcohol (以茶代酒).
- After this lesson you can say what someone is busy or in a hurry doing with 忙着 and 急着, and plan things in order with 先…再….
- After this lesson you can comment on how something is to eat, make or look at with V + 起来, and point out the obvious with 嘛.

## Grammar

### G1 · Adjective/verb + 着 + verb (busy doing, in a hurry to do)
**Pattern:** `Adj/V1 + 着 + V2 (+ O)`

Here the first part names a state or a way of behaving, typically 忙 (busy), 急 (anxious, in a hurry) or an action such as 哭 (to cry), and the verb phrase after it says what that state is about: what someone is busy with, what they are in a hurry to do, what they are crying for. 着 attaches directly to the first word, and the second verb follows straight after, with no other linking word: 忙着做饭, 急着回家, 哭着要妈妈. This is not quite the 着 you know from Level 2, which marks a lasting state (门开着) or the manner of a second action (坐着吃饭); in this pattern the second verb phrase explains why the first state exists. Learners often put 着 in the wrong place or use 在 instead.

| Chinese | Pinyin | English |
|---|---|---|
| 妈妈一下午都忙着准备年夜饭。 | Māma yí xiàwǔ dōu mángzhe zhǔnbèi niányèfàn. | Mom has been busy all afternoon getting the New Year's Eve dinner ready. |
| 他急着回家过年，连午饭都没吃就走了。 | Tā jízhe huí jiā guònián, lián wǔfàn dōu méi chī jiù zǒu le. | He was in such a hurry to get home for New Year that he left without even eating lunch. |
| 小弟弟哭着要红包。 | Xiǎo dìdi kūzhe yào hóngbāo. | My little brother was crying for a red envelope. |
| 除夕晚上，大家都忙着给朋友发短信拜年。 | Chúxī wǎnshang, dàjiā dōu mángzhe gěi péngyou fā duǎnxìn bài nián. | On New Year's Eve everyone is busy texting New Year greetings to their friends. |

**Watch out:** 着 goes right after 忙 or 急: ✗ 他忙做饭着 should be 他忙着做饭.

### G2 · The preposition 以 (with, using)
**Pattern:** `以 + N + V`

以 is a preposition handed down from classical Chinese. It means "with" or "using", and introduces the means, basis or standard by which something is done; like other prepositional phrases, 以 + noun comes before the verb. Today it lives mostly in fixed expressions and in fairly formal speech and writing. The one you will hear at every Chinese dinner table is 以茶代酒, "to replace alcohol with tea", said when someone raises a cup of tea instead of a glass of wine for a toast. Other common chunks are 以…为主 ("to take … as the main thing") and 以…的价钱 ("at a price of …"). In everyday talk about tools and means, Chinese uses 用, not 以: you eat with chopsticks 用筷子, never 以筷子. Learn the 以 phrases as chunks rather than building new ones.

| Chinese | Pinyin | English |
|---|---|---|
| 爷爷身体不好，不能喝酒，就以茶代酒了。 | Yéye shēntǐ bù hǎo, bù néng hē jiǔ, jiù yǐ chá dài jiǔ le. | Grandpa isn't well and can't drink, so he made his toast with tea instead. |
| 她以一半的价钱买到了这件衣服。 | Tā yǐ yíbàn de jiàqian mǎidàole zhè jiàn yīfu. | She got this piece of clothing at half the price. |
| 这个寒假，我们以玩为主。 | Zhège hánjià, wǒmen yǐ wán wéi zhǔ. | This winter break, we're mainly going to have fun. |

**Watch out:** for an everyday tool, use 用: ✗ 我以筷子吃饺子 should be 我用筷子吃饺子.

### G3 · The particle 嘛 (it's obvious, isn't it)
**Pattern:** `Sentence + 嘛`

嘛 is a sentence-final particle of casual conversation. It tells the listener that what you are saying is obvious, or that the reason behind it needs no explaining: "well, it's New Year, isn't it?", "he's from Beijing, of course he knows." It can also turn a suggestion into a gentle push, with a touch of impatience: if you don't know, just ask! Put it at the very end of the statement, or after a short topic like 过年嘛 before you comment on it. 嘛 is neutral-tone ma, like 吗, but it never asks a question: it states. Because it sounds familiar and sometimes a little pushy, keep it for friends and family, not for formal writing or for speaking to a boss or a teacher.

| Chinese | Pinyin | English |
|---|---|---|
| 过节嘛，大家高兴就好。 | Guòjié ma, dàjiā gāoxìng jiù hǎo. | It's a holiday. As long as everyone's happy, that's what counts. |
| 你不认识这个字，就去问老师嘛。 | Nǐ bú rènshi zhège zì, jiù qù wèn lǎoshī ma. | If you don't know this character, just go and ask the teacher. |
| 他是北京人嘛，当然知道哪儿的饺子好吃。 | Tā shì Běijīngrén ma, dāngrán zhīdào nǎr de jiǎozi hǎochī. | He's from Beijing, so of course he knows where the good dumplings are. |
| 别客气，想吃就多吃点儿嘛。 | Bié kèqi, xiǎng chī jiù duō chī diǎnr ma. | Don't be shy. If you want some, have some more! |

**Watch out:** 嘛 does not ask anything: ✗ 你是学生嘛？ should be 你是学生吗？

### G4 · The pattern (先)…再… (first … then …)
**Pattern:** `(先) + V1 + (O), 再 + V2`

先…再… lines up two actions: first do A, then do B. It is the pattern for plans, instructions and suggestions, things that have not happened yet. 再 puts a condition on the second action: B is deliberately held back until A is done. That is why 再 can stand alone after a time clause without 先 (等你来了再吃). Compare 才, which looks back at something that did happen, but later than expected (他十点才来). Both 先 and 再 are adverbs, so they go after the subject and before the verb. For a past sequence, Chinese normally uses 先… 然后… or 以后 instead. A common mistake is to use 又 for "then" in a plan.

| Chinese | Pinyin | English |
|---|---|---|
| 我们先吃年夜饭，再看春节晚会。 | Wǒmen xiān chī niányèfàn, zài kàn Chūnjié wǎnhuì. | We'll have the New Year's Eve dinner first, then watch the Spring Festival Gala. |
| 你先把作业写完，再出去玩。 | Nǐ xiān bǎ zuòyè xiěwán, zài chūqu wán. | Finish your homework first, then you can go out and play. |
| 先别吃，等大家都来了再吃。 | Xiān bié chī, děng dàjiā dōu lái le zài chī. | Don't start yet. Wait till everyone's here. |
| 我想毕业以后先工作两年，再去读研究生。 | Wǒ xiǎng bìyè yǐhòu xiān gōngzuò liǎng nián, zài qù dú yánjiūshēng. | After graduating I'd like to work for two years first, and then go to graduate school. |

**Watch out:** "then" in a plan is 再, not 又: ✗ 你先洗手，又吃饭 should be 你先洗手，再吃饭.

## Words & Phrases

### W1 · V + 起来 (when it comes to doing the verb)
起来 after a verb can mean "to start doing" (as in 冷起来), but here it means something else: "when you do this to it". The subject is the thing you judge, the verb is how you deal with it (吃, 做, 用, 看, 听, 拿), and an adjective or comment follows: 粽子吃起来很香 means "zongzi taste good when you eat them". Two such phrases are often set side by side to contrast two sides of the same thing, such as easy to eat but hard to make. Pinyin writes 起来 here in the neutral tone.

| Chinese | Pinyin | English |
|---|---|---|
| 粽子吃起来很香，可是包起来不容易。 | Zòngzi chī qilai hěn xiāng, kěshì bāo qilai bù róngyì. | Zongzi taste great, but they're not easy to wrap. |
| 这个字看起来很难，其实写起来不难。 | Zhège zì kàn qǐlai hěn nán, qíshí xiě qilai bù nán. | This character looks hard, but it's actually not hard to write. |
| 这个箱子拿起来不重。 | Zhège xiāngzi ná qilai bú zhòng. | This suitcase isn't heavy to carry. |

**Watch out:** the thing you judge is the subject: ✗ 我吃起来饺子很好吃 should be 饺子吃起来很香.

### W2 · 传统 (tradition; traditional)
传统 is both an adjective and a noun. As an adjective it often goes straight before a noun without 的 (传统节日, 传统文化), though 的 is also possible (传统的想法). As a predicate it takes a degree word and describes ideas or people: 我爷爷很传统 means he holds old-fashioned views. As a noun it means "a tradition", often with 有 and an evaluating word in front: 我们家有一个好传统. Once a degree word like 很 comes in front, 的 is needed before the noun.

| Chinese | Pinyin | English |
|---|---|---|
| 春节是中国最重要的传统节日。 | Chūnjié shì Zhōngguó zuì zhòngyào de chuántǒng jiérì. | The Spring Festival is China's most important traditional holiday. |
| 我爷爷的想法很传统。 | Wǒ yéye de xiǎngfǎ hěn chuántǒng. | My grandfather's views are very traditional. |
| 我们家有一个好传统：每年除夕一家人一起包饺子。 | Wǒmen jiā yǒu yí ge hǎo chuántǒng: měi nián chúxī yì jiā rén yìqǐ bāo jiǎozi. | My family has a nice tradition: every New Year's Eve we all make dumplings together. |

**Watch out:** after 很, 的 is needed: ✗ 这是一个很传统节日 should be 这是一个很传统的节日.

### W3 · 热闹 (lively, bustling)
热闹 is an adjective for a place, a scene or an occasion full of people, noise and activity: a market, a street on New Year's Eve, a family dinner. It can be the predicate (街上很热闹) or come before a noun with 的 (热闹的地方). It also works like a verb, "to liven things up, to have a good time together", usually reduplicated as 热闹热闹 or with 一下. For most Chinese speakers 热闹 is a warm, positive word, but it can also suggest crowds and noise, so some people say they do not like places that are too 热闹. It describes situations, not a person's feelings.

| Chinese | Pinyin | English |
|---|---|---|
| 除夕晚上，街上特别热闹。 | Chúxī wǎnshang, jiē shang tèbié rènao. | On New Year's Eve the streets are really lively. |
| 周末请几个朋友来家里热闹热闹吧。 | Zhōumò qǐng jǐ ge péngyou lái jiā li rènao rènao ba. | Let's invite a few friends over this weekend and have a good time. |
| 我不喜欢太热闹的地方，人太多了。 | Wǒ bù xǐhuan tài rènao de dìfang, rén tài duō le. | I don't like places that are too busy: there are too many people. |

**Watch out:** 热闹 describes a place or event, not a feeling: ✗ 我今天很热闹 should be 我今天很高兴.

## Exercises

### E1 · Choose the right word {type=choice}
1. 吃不了就别点那么多菜，太___了。 [顺利 | 浪费 | 热闹] → 浪费
2. 这个字看___很难，可是写起来很容易。 [上来 | 下来 | 起来] → 起来
3. 妈妈一下午都忙___准备年夜饭。 [着 | 了 | 过] → 着
4. 我们先吃饭，___看春节晚会。 [才 | 再 | 又] → 再
5. 你不知道怎么走，就问问别人___！ [吗 | 呢 | 嘛] → 嘛
6. 我不能喝酒，就___茶代酒吧。 [给 | 以 | 被] → 以
7. 春节的时候，外边到处都是人，非常___。 [热闹 | 传统 | 成功] → 热闹
8. 我爷爷很___，他觉得过年一定要回家团圆。 [顺利 | 热闹 | 传统] → 传统

### E2 · Put the words in order {type=order}
1. 年夜饭 / 正忙着 / 妈妈 / 做 → 妈妈正忙着做年夜饭。
2. 倒着 / 他 / 贴在门上了 / 把福字 → 他把福字倒着贴在门上了。
3. 吃起来 / 月饼 / 太甜了 / 这种 → 这种月饼吃起来太甜了。
4. 再去拜年 / 吃了早饭 / 我们 / 先 → 我们先吃了早饭再去拜年。
5. 环境 / 真 / 这个小区的 / 好 → 这个小区的环境真好。
6. 不能 / 他开车 / 喝酒 / 所以 → 他开车，所以不能喝酒。
7. 才剩下的 / 这条鱼 / 是为了 / 年年有余 → 这条鱼是为了年年有余才剩下的。

### E3 · Translate into Chinese {type=translate}
1. Let's toast with tea instead of alcohol. Cheers! → 我们以茶代酒，干杯！
2. I wish you smooth work and good health in the new year. → 祝你新年工作顺利，身体健康！
3. He was in a hurry to go home for New Year, so he didn't eat lunch. → 他急着回家过年，所以没吃午饭。
4. Let's watch the Spring Festival Gala first and then make dumplings. → 我们先看春节晚会，再包饺子吧。
5. The Dragon Boat Festival is on the fifth day of the fifth lunar month. → 端午节是农历五月初五。
6. At the Mid-Autumn Festival the whole family gets together and eats moon cakes. → 中秋节的时候，一家人团圆，一起吃月饼。
7. These dumplings aren't wrapped very nicely, but they taste great. → 这些饺子包得不太好看，可是吃起来很香。

### E4 · Match the word {type=match}
1. 除夕 → Chinese New Year's Eve
2. 年夜饭 → Chinese New Year's Eve dinner
3. 贴 → to paste, to glue
4. 浪费 → to waste
5. 团圆 → to reunite (as a family)
6. 拜年 → to wish somebody a happy Chinese New Year
7. 红包 → red envelope containing gift money
8. 鞭炮 → firecracker
