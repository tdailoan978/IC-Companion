# Lesson 9 · 面试 · Job Interview

## Objectives
- After this lesson you can explain why multinational companies and talented people have been drawn to China, and what returnees from overseas are nicknamed.
- After this lesson you can prepare for a job interview and answer typical questions about yourself, your strengths and weaknesses, and your plans.
- After this lesson you can say that two things change together with 越X越Y, and draw a conclusion from a known fact with 既然.
- After this lesson you can describe something that happens in the middle of another action with V着V着.
- After this lesson you can point out a lucky circumstance with 好在, say what someone is good at with 善于, and choose between 往往 and 常常.

## Grammar

### G1 · 越X越Y (the more X, the more Y)
**Pattern:** `(Subj) + 越 + X + (Subj) + 越 + Y`

越X越Y says that Y changes in step with X: the more you do one thing, or the more one thing is true, the more the second follows. X and Y are verbs, verb phrases or adjectives, and each 越 stands directly in front of its own part. When both halves share one subject, it comes once at the front (雨越下越大). When the halves have different subjects, each subject goes before its own 越 (你越说，他越生气), and 就 may join the second half. A very common short form gives advice: 越早越好, "the earlier the better". You already know 越来越, which only says that something changes over time; 越X越Y names what drives the change. Because 越 itself means "more", never add 很, 更 or 非常 in front of Y.

| Chinese | Pinyin | English |
|---|---|---|
| 外边的雨越下越大，我们还是别出去了。 | Wàibian de yǔ yuè xià yuè dà, wǒmen háishi bié chūqu le. | The rain outside is getting heavier and heavier; we'd better not go out. |
| 这本小说我越看越喜欢。 | Zhè běn xiǎoshuō wǒ yuè kàn yuè xǐhuan. | The more I read this novel, the more I like it. |
| 你越跟他解释，他就越不高兴。 | Nǐ yuè gēn tā jiěshì, tā jiù yuè bù gāoxìng. | The more you explain to him, the more upset he gets. |
| 准备面试，越早越好。 | Zhǔnbèi miànshì, yuè zǎo yuè hǎo. | When it comes to preparing for an interview, the earlier the better. |

**Watch out:** no degree adverb after the second 越: ✗ 他的中文越说越很好 should be 他的中文越说越好.

### G2 · The conjunction 既然 (since, now that)
**Pattern:** `既然 + known fact, (Subj) + 就/那么 + conclusion`

既然 opens the first clause and restates something both speakers already know, because it has just been said or is plain to see. The second clause then draws the natural conclusion: a suggestion, a decision, or a question such as "then why …?". That second clause very often has 就 after its subject, or starts with 那(么). The difference from 因为 is important. 因为 can bring in a reason the listener has not heard before; 既然 can only build on a fact that is already shared, which is why it often follows something the other person has just told you. For the same reason a 既然 clause cannot answer a 为什么 question, and it cannot introduce a guess about the future.

| Chinese | Pinyin | English |
|---|---|---|
| 既然你不舒服，今天就别去上课了。 | Jìrán nǐ bù shūfu, jīntiān jiù bié qù shàng kè le. | Since you're not feeling well, don't go to class today. |
| 既然大家都同意，那我们就这么办吧。 | Jìrán dàjiā dōu tóngyì, nà wǒmen jiù zhème bàn ba. | Since everyone agrees, let's do it this way. |
| 既然你这么喜欢北京，干吗不去那儿找工作？ | Jìrán nǐ zhème xǐhuan Běijīng, gànmá bú qù nàr zhǎo gōngzuò? | Since you like Beijing so much, why not look for a job there? |
| 既然公司已经录用了你，你就好好工作吧。 | Jìrán gōngsī yǐjīng lùyòngle nǐ, nǐ jiù hǎohǎo gōngzuò ba. | Now that the company has taken you on, do a good job. |

**Watch out:** a new reason given in an answer takes 因为: ✗ 你为什么没来？既然我病了 should be 因为我病了.

### G3 · V着V着 (while doing something, …)
**Pattern:** `V + 着 + V + 着, (Subj) + (就) + new action or result`

Saying a verb twice with 着 paints an action that goes on for a while, and the clause after it tells what happened in the middle of it, usually without anyone planning it: you were reading and fell asleep, you were chatting and it got dark, you kept practising and got better. The verb is normally a single syllable with no object (看着看着, 走着走着, 聊着聊着). The second half is required, since V着V着 only sets the scene, and it often contains 就 before the verb, though 就 can be dropped. Plain V着 describes a state or the way something is done (坐着说); doubling it adds the idea of time passing until something changes.

| Chinese | Pinyin | English |
|---|---|---|
| 他看着看着就睡着了。 | Tā kànzhe kànzhe jiù shuìzháo le. | He was reading and dozed off. |
| 我们聊着聊着，天就黑了。 | Wǒmen liáozhe liáozhe, tiān jiù hēi le. | We were chatting away, and before we knew it, it was dark. |
| 她说着说着就笑了。 | Tā shuōzhe shuōzhe jiù xiào le. | She was in the middle of talking when she burst out laughing. |
| 他练着练着，中文就越来越好了。 | Tā liànzhe liànzhe, Zhōngwén jiù yuè lái yuè hǎo le. | He kept on practising, and his Chinese got better and better. |

**Watch out:** V着V着 cannot end the sentence: ✗ 他想着想着。 should be 他想着想着就笑了。

## Words & Phrases

### W1 · 叫做 (to be called, to be known as)
叫做 is a verb plus complement that gives the name or term by which something or someone is known. It sounds more formal than plain 叫, so it suits explanations of terms, nicknames and names used in a region. It most often appears in two frames: 把 + thing + 叫做 + "name" (people call it …), and thing + 被 + 叫做 + "name" (it is called …). The name usually stands in quotation marks. For your own name or a friend's ordinary name, use 叫, not 叫做: 叫做 implies that a label has been given, often with a reason behind it.

| Chinese | Pinyin | English |
|---|---|---|
| 他个子特别高，所以朋友们把他叫做“长城”。 | Tā gèzi tèbié gāo, suǒyǐ péngyoumen bǎ tā jiào zuò "Chángchéng". | He's very tall, so his friends call him "the Great Wall". |
| 我们学校的图书馆二十四小时开门，学生们把它叫做“第二个宿舍”。 | Wǒmen xuéxiào de túshūguǎn èrshísì xiǎoshí kāi mén, xuéshengmen bǎ tā jiào zuò "dì èr ge sùshè". | Our school library is open twenty-four hours, and the students call it "the second dorm". |
| 这种水果在南方被叫做什么？ | Zhè zhǒng shuǐguǒ zài nánfāng bèi jiào zuò shénme? | What is this kind of fruit called in the south? |

**Watch out:** an ordinary personal name takes 叫: ✗ 我叫做马克 should be 我叫马克.

### W2 · 好在 (fortunately, luckily)
好在 is an adverb that points to the one fact that saves an otherwise bad or risky situation. The bad news comes first, and 好在 opens the next clause, the one that states the lucky circumstance: it rained, but luckily I had an umbrella. It stands at the very start of that clause, in front of the subject if there is one. A third clause starting with 要不然 often follows to say what would have happened without that luck. 好在 is common in speech and writing alike. It never introduces the bad part of the story, and it is not used for good news that has no problem in the background.

| Chinese | Pinyin | English |
|---|---|---|
| 昨天突然下雨了，好在我带了伞，要不然衣服就全湿了。 | Zuótiān tūrán xià yǔ le, hǎozài wǒ dàile sǎn, yàobùrán yīfu jiù quán shī le. | It suddenly rained yesterday. Luckily I had an umbrella, or my clothes would have got soaked. |
| 今天路上车特别多，好在我出门出得早，没有迟到。 | Jīntiān lù shang chē tèbié duō, hǎozài wǒ chū mén chū de zǎo, méiyǒu chídào. | There was heavy traffic today, but luckily I left early and wasn't late. |
| 姐姐明天就要面试了，好在她上个星期刚买了一身新套装。 | Jiějie míngtiān jiù yào miànshì le, hǎozài tā shàng ge xīngqī gāng mǎile yì shēn xīn tàozhuāng. | My sister has her interview tomorrow; luckily she bought a new suit just last week. |

**Watch out:** 好在 goes with the lucky fact, not the bad one: ✗ 好在下雨了，我带了伞 should be 下雨了，好在我带了伞.

### W3 · 善于 (to be good at, to be adept in)
善于 is a fairly formal verb meaning "to be skilled at", and it takes a verb phrase as its object: 善于学习, 善于解释, 善于跟人打交道. It is common in descriptions of people's abilities, such as job interviews, recommendations and reports. It can be modified by 很 or 特别 and negated with 不 (不善于, "not good at"). In casual talk people more often say 很会 + verb (她很会说话) or verb + 得很好. Because its object is an activity, a bare noun such as a language or a subject sounds wrong after 善于: say what the person does well.

| Chinese | Pinyin | English |
|---|---|---|
| 她很善于跟人打交道，做销售很合适。 | Tā hěn shànyú gēn rén dǎ jiāodao, zuò xiāoshòu hěn héshì. | She's very good at dealing with people, so sales suits her well. |
| 我不善于在很多人面前说话。 | Wǒ bú shànyú zài hěn duō rén miànqián shuō huà. | I'm not good at speaking in front of lots of people. |
| 王老师特别善于解释难的语法。 | Wáng lǎoshī tèbié shànyú jiěshì nán de yǔfǎ. | Teacher Wang is especially good at explaining difficult grammar. |

**Watch out:** the object is an activity, not a bare noun: ✗ 他很善于中文 should be 他很善于学外语.

### W4 · 往往 vs. 常常 (more often than not vs. often)
Both are adverbs of frequency that stand before the verb, but they look at frequency differently. 往往 sums up a tendency drawn from past experience: under certain conditions, this is what usually happens. So a 往往 sentence normally names the condition (周末, 一紧张, 刚来的时候) and describes a pattern, not a single plan. It cannot refer to the future and cannot be used in requests or wishes. 常常 just says that something happens often, with or without a condition, and it works for the past, present and future, including invitations: 以后常常来玩儿. When in doubt, 常常 is the safer word; 往往 sounds more like an observation or a general rule.

| Chinese | Pinyin | English |
|---|---|---|
| 周末的时候，这家饭馆往往要等半个小时。 | Zhōumò de shíhou, zhè jiā fànguǎn wǎngwǎng yào děng bàn ge xiǎoshí. | At weekends there's usually a half-hour wait at this restaurant. |
| 他一紧张，往往就说不出话来。 | Tā yì jǐnzhāng, wǎngwǎng jiù shuō bu chū huà lai. | When he gets nervous, more often than not he can't get a word out. |
| 我常常在图书馆看书。 | Wǒ chángcháng zài túshūguǎn kàn shū. | I often read in the library. |
| 欢迎你以后常常来我家玩儿。 | Huānyíng nǐ yǐhòu chángcháng lái wǒ jiā wánr. | You're welcome to come round to my place often. |

**Watch out:** requests and future plans take 常常: ✗ 以后请你往往给我打电话 should be 以后请你常常给我打电话.

### Dialogue 2 · The interview

## Exercises

### E1 · Choose the right word {type=choice}
1. 这个菜我越吃___喜欢。 [更 | 越 | 很] → 越
2. ___你已经决定了，我就不再说什么了。 [既然 | 虽然 | 不但] → 既然
3. 弟弟玩着玩___就忘了吃饭。 [了 | 过 | 着] → 着
4. 这次考试很难，___我准备得很好。 [往往 | 好在 | 既然] → 好在
5. 毕业以后，我们也要___见面啊。 [往往 | 好在 | 常常] → 常常
6. 每次考试以前，他___紧张得睡不着觉。 [往往 | 好在 | 既然] → 往往
7. 她很___安排时间，所以从来不用加班。 [满意 | 吸引 | 善于] → 善于
8. 大家把从海外回来的留学生___"海龟"。 [叫做 | 回答 | 解释] → 叫做

### E2 · Put the words in order {type=order}
1. 越想吃 / 这种零食 / 越吃 / 真是 → 这种零食真是越吃越想吃。
2. 就 / 既然你也想去 / 一起去吧 / 我们 → 既然你也想去，我们就一起去吧。
3. 讲着讲着 / 睡着了 / 奶奶 / 就 → 奶奶讲着讲着就睡着了。
4. 寄给我了 / 公司 / 录用通知 / 把 → 公司把录用通知寄给我了。
5. 好在 / 我的上衣 / 裤子没湿 / 都湿了 → 我的上衣都湿了，好在裤子没湿。
6. 跟我 / 总经理 / 握手 / 站起来 → 总经理站起来跟我握手。
7. 做饭 / 善于 / 不 / 我爸爸 → 我爸爸不善于做饭。

### E3 · Translate into Chinese {type=translate}
1. The more people, the merrier. → 人越多越热闹。
2. Since the manager is satisfied with you, don't worry. → 既然经理对你很满意，你就别担心了。
3. They chatted and chatted and became friends. → 他们聊着聊着就成了朋友。
4. Luckily my cousin lives in Beijing; otherwise I wouldn't know anyone there. → 好在我表哥住在北京，要不然我在那儿一个人都不认识。
5. My biggest weakness is that I love snacks too much. → 我最大的缺点是太爱吃零食了。
6. In an interview, the manager more often than not asks about your strengths and weaknesses. → 面试的时候，经理往往会问你的优点和缺点。
7. The company will let you know next week. → 公司下个星期会通知你。

1. 世纪 → century
