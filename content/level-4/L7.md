# Lesson 7 · 理财与投资 · Wealth Management and Investing

## Objectives
- After this lesson you can say whether you are a saver or a spender and describe your own spending habits.
- After this lesson you can talk about ways to invest money (savings accounts, stocks, property) and weigh up their risks.
- After this lesson you can discuss how to pay for a big purchase such as a flat: savings, help from family, a mortgage.
- After this lesson you can say what someone does with a particular sum of money using 把, and describe a relaxed daily routine with doubled verbs.
- After this lesson you can describe sudden ups and downs with 突然, say what something counts as with 算(是), and talk about what an event leads to with 引起.

## Grammar

### G1 · The 把 construction (review I): doing something to a known thing
**Pattern:** `Subj + 把 + known noun + V + complement / 了 / doubled verb`

You met 把 in Level 2. This review sharpens two rules. First, the noun after 把 must be something both speakers can identify: 这笔钱, 你的存款, 那份合同. If it is new and unspecific (一本书), the listener will ask "which one?", so use an ordinary sentence instead. Second, the verb cannot stand bare. It needs something after it that shows what happened to the thing: a result or direction complement (存起来, 放进股市, 花完), a 了 that often means the thing is gone (把工资花了, 把股票卖了), or a doubled verb for a quick, casual action (把合同看看). 把 is natural whenever you talk about handling, moving or using up money and objects. The typical errors are a bare verb after the object and 把 with an indefinite noun.

| Chinese | Pinyin | English |
|---|---|---|
| 你先把这笔钱存起来，以后用得着。 | Nǐ xiān bǎ zhè bǐ qián cún qilai, yǐhòu yòng de zháo. | Put this money away for now; you'll need it later. |
| 他把三年的存款都放进了股市。 | Tā bǎ sān nián de cúnkuǎn dōu fàngjìnle gǔshì. | He put three years' savings into the stock market. |
| 签以前，你最好把合同看看。 | Qiān yǐqián, nǐ zuìhǎo bǎ hétong kànkan. | Before you sign, you'd better take a look at the contract. |
| 糟糕，我把这个月的工资都花了！ | Zāogāo, wǒ bǎ zhège yuè de gōngzī dōu huā le! | Oh no, I've already spent this month's pay! |

**Watch out:** the verb needs something after it, and the object must be specific: ✗ 我把这笔钱存 should be 我把这笔钱存起来了; ✗ 我把一本书买了 should be 我买了一本书.

### G2 · Doubled verbs (II): a relaxed, habitual routine
**Pattern:** `(time word) + V V (O), V V (O) …` (散散步, 喝喝茶, 打打麻将)

Until now a doubled verb (看看, 试试) softened a suggestion about one action in the near future: "have a look", "give it a try". Doubled verbs can also describe things someone does regularly, at no fixed moment, especially easy pastimes. Two or three of them often come in a row, and together they paint a leisurely, unhurried life: 每天散散步、喝喝茶. This use is common when describing retired people, weekends or holidays. With a verb-object word only the verb is doubled (散散步, 聊聊天, 打打球), never the whole word. In this habitual use the doubled verb takes no 了, 过 or complement (a single finished action is V了V: 散了散步), and in a list like this it does not mean "just this once".

| Chinese | Pinyin | English |
|---|---|---|
| 我爷爷退休以后，每天看看书，喝喝茶，过得很舒服。 | Wǒ yéye tuìxiū yǐhòu, měitiān kànkan shū, hēhe chá, guò de hěn shūfu. | Since my grandpa retired he reads a bit and drinks a bit of tea every day, and he's living very comfortably. |
| 那几个老太太每天下午在公园里打打麻将，聊聊天。 | Nà jǐ ge lǎotàitai měitiān xiàwǔ zài gōngyuán li dǎda májiàng, liáoliao tiān. | Those old ladies play a little mahjong and chat in the park every afternoon. |
| 放假的时候，我就在家睡睡懒觉，看看电影。 | Fàngjià de shíhou, wǒ jiù zài jiā shuìshui lǎnjiào, kànkan diànyǐng. | On holidays I just stay home, sleep in and watch a few movies. |
| 她周末常常去医院做做义工，帮帮病人。 | Tā zhōumò chángcháng qù yīyuàn zuòzuo yìgōng, bāngbang bìngrén. | At weekends she often does some volunteer work at the hospital, helping the patients. |

**Watch out:** double only the verb part of a verb-object word, and leave out 了: ✗ 他每天散步散步 should be 他每天散散步; ✗ 他昨天散散步了 should be 他昨天散了散步.

## Words & Phrases

### W1 · 引起 (to give rise to, to lead to)
引起 is a verb. Its subject is the thing that sets something off (a remark, an event, rising prices), and its object is the reaction it produces, almost always a two-syllable abstract noun: 注意, 兴趣, 讨论, 担心, 思考, 矛盾. The person who reacts is usually attached to the object with 的: 引起了大家的注意, 引起了我的兴趣. 了 often follows because the reaction has already happened. 引起 sounds a little more formal than 让, and it cannot take a whole clause after it: to say "made me go and do something" use 让.

| Chinese | Pinyin | English |
|---|---|---|
| 公寓的价钱越来越高，引起了很多人的担心。 | Gōngyù de jiàqián yuè lái yuè gāo, yǐnqǐle hěn duō rén de dānxīn. | Flats keep getting more expensive, and that has a lot of people worried. |
| 老师的问题引起了大家的讨论。 | Lǎoshī de wèntí yǐnqǐle dàjiā de tǎolùn. | The teacher's question got everyone discussing. |
| 这本书引起了我对中国历史的兴趣。 | Zhè běn shū yǐnqǐle wǒ duì Zhōngguó lìshǐ de xìngqù. | This book got me interested in Chinese history. |

**Watch out:** 引起 takes a noun, not a clause: ✗ 他的话引起我去银行存钱 should be 他的话让我去银行存钱.

### W2 · 算(是) (to count as, to be considered as)
The basic meaning of 算 is "to count, to calculate" (算算一个月花多少钱). In this lesson it means "can be counted as, may be regarded as", and 是 after it is optional. 算(是) is looser than plain 是: it says that something can be put in a category, more or less, or that we agree to treat it in a certain way, for example calling money a loan or a gift. The negative is 不算 (不算贵, 不算多), which is a polite way of saying "not really". 算是 also sounds modest when you describe yourself: 我算是会做饭吧.

| Chinese | Pinyin | English |
|---|---|---|
| 这五百块算是我借给你的，你以后再还我。 | Zhè wǔbǎi kuài suàn shì wǒ jiè gěi nǐ de, nǐ yǐhòu zài huán wǒ. | Let's call this five hundred a loan from me; pay me back later. |
| 他在北京住了十年，算是半个北京人了。 | Tā zài Běijīng zhùle shí nián, suàn shì bàn ge Běijīngrén le. | He's lived in Beijing for ten years, so he's practically half a Beijinger. |
| 这次投资不算成功，不过也没赔钱。 | Zhè cì tóuzī bú suàn chénggōng, búguò yě méi péi qián. | This investment wasn't really a success, but at least I didn't lose money. |
| 一个月存三百块，算多吗？ | Yí ge yuè cún sānbǎi kuài, suàn duō ma? | Is saving three hundred a month a lot? |

**Watch out:** when something simply is so, use 是, not 算是: ✗ 他算是我哥哥 (about your real brother) should be 他是我哥哥.

### W3 · 合 (to combine, to join; together)
合 means to put together or to share. On its own it is used with 在一起 (把两个人的钱合在一起). More often it stands in front of a one-syllable verb and turns it into "do it jointly with others": 合租 (rent together), 合用 (share the use of), 合写 (co-write), 合唱 (sing together), 合开 (open a business together), 合买 (buy jointly). The partners come first, often with 跟 or 和: 我跟朋友合租一套公寓. These compounds are short and fixed, so 合 does not attach to just any verb or phrase; for "together" in general use 一起.

| Chinese | Pinyin | English |
|---|---|---|
| 我跟两个同学合租了一套公寓，房租便宜多了。 | Wǒ gēn liǎng ge tóngxué hézūle yí tào gōngyù, fángzū piányi duō le. | I rent a flat together with two classmates, and the rent is much cheaper. |
| 我和同屋合用一台电脑。 | Wǒ hé tóngwū héyòng yì tái diànnǎo. | My roommate and I share a computer. |
| 他跟朋友合开了一家饭馆。 | Tā gēn péngyou hékāile yì jiā fànguǎn. | He opened a restaurant with a friend. |

**Watch out:** for doing ordinary things together use 一起, not 合: ✗ 我们合吃饭吧 should be 我们一起吃饭吧.

### W4 · 突然 (sudden, unexpected; suddenly)
突然 is an adjective that is also used as an adverb. Before a verb it means "suddenly" (股票突然跌了). After a subject, usually with 很 or 太, it is the predicate: 这个消息太突然了 (the news is too sudden). After 得 it is a complement describing the way something happened: 这件事发生得很突然. In all three positions it describes something that comes without warning. The near-synonym 忽然 is only an adverb, so it can stand before a verb but cannot be the predicate or follow 得.

| Chinese | Pinyin | English |
|---|---|---|
| 我们正在聊天，他突然站起来走了。 | Wǒmen zhèngzài liáotiān, tā tūrán zhàn qilai zǒu le. | We were chatting when he suddenly got up and left. |
| 你要搬家？这也太突然了！ | Nǐ yào bānjiā? Zhè yě tài tūrán le! | You're moving? That's so sudden! |
| 这件事发生得太突然了，大家都没想到。 | Zhè jiàn shì fāshēng de tài tūrán le, dàjiā dōu méi xiǎngdào. | It happened so suddenly that nobody saw it coming. |

**Watch out:** only 突然 can be the predicate: ✗ 这件事很忽然 should be 这件事很突然.

## Exercises

### E1 · Choose the right word {type=choice}
1. 你先___这笔钱存进银行吧。 [从 | 把 | 对] → 把
2. 他昨天说的话___了大家的讨论。 [引起 | 增加 | 享受] → 引起
3. 这两百块___是我送你的生日礼物，不用还了。 [劝 | 合 | 算] → 算
4. 我想跟朋友___租一套公寓，这样房租能便宜一点儿。 [合 | 算 | 签] → 合
5. 他昨天说下个月要去中国工作，这个消息太___了！ [辛苦 | 节俭 | 突然] → 突然
6. 周末的时候，我常常跟朋友打打球，___天。 [聊了 | 聊过 | 聊聊] → 聊聊
7. 股票突然跌了，他把三年的存款都___。 [赔了 | 赔 | 赔过] → 赔了
8. 他买的股票昨天涨了，今天又___了。 [跌 | 签 | 存] → 跌

### E2 · Put the words in order {type=order}
1. 存进了 / 我 / 银行 / 把这笔钱 → 我把这笔钱存进了银行。
2. 大家的讨论 / 引起了 / 他的话 → 他的话引起了大家的讨论。
3. 合租 / 我想 / 一套公寓 / 跟朋友 → 我想跟朋友合租一套公寓。
4. 吧 / 去公园 / 走走 / 我们 → 我们去公园走走吧。
5. 算是 / 吧 / 这些钱 / 我借给你的 → 这些钱算是我借给你的吧。
6. 放进股市 / 我劝他 / 存款 / 别把 → 我劝他别把存款放进股市。
7. 再签 / 合同 / 看看 / 先把 → 先把合同看看再签。

### E3 · Translate into Chinese {type=translate}
1. I've decided to put half of my pay into the bank every month. → 我决定每个月把一半的工资存进银行。
2. Stocks carry big risks; you might lose money. → 股票的风险很大，你可能会赔钱。
3. Her words got me thinking. → 她的话引起了我的思考。
4. Since retiring, my grandma plays a little mahjong and drinks some tea every day. → 我奶奶退休以后，每天打打麻将，喝喝茶。
5. This money counts as a present from my parents. → 这笔钱算是我爸爸妈妈送我的礼物。
6. The price of that flat suddenly went up, and he felt very gloomy. → 那套公寓的价钱突然涨了，他觉得很郁闷。
7. My friends persuaded me to be a bit more thrifty. → 朋友们说服我节俭一点儿。

### E4 · Match the word {type=match}
1. 节俭 → thrifty, frugal
2. 利息 → interest (on savings)
3. 股市 → stock market
4. 风险 → risk
5. 合同 → contract
6. 姑妈 → aunt (father's sister)
7. 义工 → volunteer work
8. 麻将 → mahjong
