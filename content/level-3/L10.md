# Lesson 10 · 中国地理 · Geography of China

## Objectives
- After this lesson you can find China's main cities, provinces, rivers and landforms on a map and say where they are with 东部, 西北 and the like.
- After this lesson you can compare China with your own country in terrain, area and population, and set two contrasting facts side by side with 而 and 呢.
- After this lesson you can say that a change is starting or settling down with 起来 and 下来.
- After this lesson you can recommend a destination with 最…不过了 and back up your advice with past experience using 过.
- After this lesson you can weigh the pros and cons of different places and plan a travel route, giving purposes with 为了 and reasons with 因为.

## Grammar

### G1 · 起来 and 下来: a state begins or settles (revisited)
**Pattern:** `Subj + V / Adj + 起来 (了)` · with an object: `V + 起 + Obj + 来` · `V / Adj + 下来 (了)`

You met 起来 in Level 2 for an action that starts and goes on (笑起来, 下起雨来). The new step is to use it freely after adjectives to say that a state is setting in, usually a rise of some kind: busier, hotter, colder, more crowded. That makes it handy for weather and seasons (天气冷起来了) and for places that come to life (人多起来了). 下来 is its mirror image: after an adjective or verb it marks a decrease or a settling down, such as getting dark, quiet or slow, or coming to a stop. The object still splits 起来 into 起 + object + 来. Learners tend to put the object after the whole complement, or to use 起来 for a calming change where 下来 is needed.

| Chinese | Pinyin | English |
|---|---|---|
| 快过节了，大家都忙起来了。 | Kuài guòjié le, dàjiā dōu máng qilai le. | The holiday is almost here, and everyone is getting busy. |
| 我们一打开地图，就聊起寒假的路线来了。 | Wǒmen yì dǎkāi dìtú, jiù liáo qi hánjià de lùxiàn lai le. | As soon as we opened the map, we started talking about our winter-break route. |
| 六点以后，天慢慢黑下来了。 | Liù diǎn yǐhòu, tiān mànmàn hēi xialai le. | After six o'clock it slowly grew dark. |
| 老师一进来，教室里就安静下来了。 | Lǎoshī yí jìnlai, jiàoshì li jiù ānjìng xialai le. | As soon as the teacher came in, the classroom went quiet. |

**Watch out:** the object goes between 起 and 来: ✗ 他们聊起来天了 should be 他们聊起天来了.

### G2 · The conjunction 而 (while, whereas)
**Pattern:** `Clause 1，而 + Clause 2`

而 joins two clauses that describe different or opposite situations, much like English "while" or "whereas": one place is cold, while another is warm; one person likes this, whereas another prefers that. The second clause usually has its own subject, so the contrast is between two topics. 而 can also set a chosen option against a rejected one: 不…，而… ("not this, but that"). It sounds a little more written and thoughtful than 可是, and it is ideal for comparing regions, people or countries. What 而 cannot do is join two nouns or simply add one fact to another; for plain "and", use 和 between nouns and 也 or 还 between clauses.

| Chinese | Pinyin | English |
|---|---|---|
| 哈尔滨冬天冷得不得了，而广州冬天一点儿也不冷。 | Hā'ěrbīn dōngtiān lěng de bùdéliǎo, ér Guǎngzhōu dōngtiān yìdiǎnr yě bù lěng. | Harbin is bitterly cold in winter, while Guangzhou isn't cold at all. |
| 我哥哥喜欢大城市，而我更喜欢安静的小地方。 | Wǒ gēge xǐhuan dà chéngshì, ér wǒ gèng xǐhuan ānjìng de xiǎo dìfang. | My brother likes big cities, whereas I prefer quiet little places. |
| 他这个寒假不回家，而去云南旅游。 | Tā zhège hánjià bù huí jiā, ér qù Yúnnán lǚyóu. | This winter break he isn't going home; he's going on a trip to Yunnan instead. |

**Watch out:** 而 does not join nouns: ✗ 我去过北京而上海 should be 我去过北京和上海.

### G3 · 最 + adjective + 不过了 (nothing could be more…)
**Pattern:** `X + 最 + Adj + 不过了`

This is an emphatic way of saying that X is as good, as clear or as suitable as anything can be: literally, "nothing goes beyond X in being adjective". The adjective sits between 最 and 不过了, and 了 closes the phrase. It is mostly used with positive judging words such as 好, 合适, 方便 or 清楚, so it works well when you give a firm recommendation or settle an argument: "for that, X is perfect". Compare plain 最好 or 最合适, which only rank X first; 最…不过了 adds the speaker's strong conviction. Learners sometimes swap 最 for 很, or drop 了.

| Chinese | Pinyin | English |
|---|---|---|
| 去南京坐火车最方便不过了。 | Qù Nánjīng zuò huǒchē zuì fāngbiàn búguò le. | Going to Nanjing, nothing beats taking the train. |
| 他家乡的事，他最清楚不过了。 | Tā jiāxiāng de shì, tā zuì qīngchu búguò le. | When it comes to his hometown, nobody knows more than he does. |
| 你想了解少数民族的生活？问她最合适不过了，她是云南人。 | Nǐ xiǎng liǎojiě shǎoshù mínzú de shēnghuó? Wèn tā zuì héshì búguò le, tā shì Yúnnán rén. | You want to learn about the life of ethnic minorities? She's the perfect person to ask; she's from Yunnan. |

**Watch out:** the pattern needs 最, not 很: ✗ 坐火车很方便不过了 should be 坐火车最方便不过了.

### G4 · The particle 过 revisited: experience as background
**Pattern:** `Subj + V + 过 (+ Obj)，explanation clause`

In Level 2 you used 过 for "have ever done". At this level, notice how it differs from 了. 了 tells a story: an event happened, and usually we know when (我昨天去了图书馆). 过 gives background: it says that you have had an experience at some vague time in the past, and that this experience explains or supports what comes next (我去过新疆，所以知道那儿多冷). Normally no time phrase is needed with 过; you add one only when you want to prove a point or contradict someone ("I really did go there, last January"). In this experiential sense, 过 is not followed by 了, and the negative is 没(有)…过.

| Chinese | Pinyin | English |
|---|---|---|
| 我去过新疆，那儿的沙漠大得不得了。 | Wǒ qùguo Xīnjiāng, nàr de shāmò dà de bùdéliǎo. | I've been to Xinjiang; the deserts there are enormous. |
| 他学过中国地理，你可以问问他。 | Tā xuéguo Zhōngguó dìlǐ, nǐ kěyǐ wènwen tā. | He has studied Chinese geography, so you can ask him. |
| 我在长江上坐过船，两边的风景特别漂亮。 | Wǒ zài Chángjiāng shang zuòguo chuán, liǎng biān de fēngjǐng tèbié piàoliang. | I've taken a boat on the Yangtze; the scenery on both banks is beautiful. |
| 我去年一月真的去过哈尔滨，你看，这是我的照片。 | Wǒ qùnián yī yuè zhēn de qùguo Hā'ěrbīn, nǐ kàn, zhè shì wǒ de zhàopiàn. | I really did go to Harbin last January. Look, here are my photos. |

**Watch out:** no 了 after experiential 过: ✗ 我去过了云南 should be 我去过云南.

## Words & Phrases

### W1 · 为了 and 因为 (for the purpose of; because)
Both words answer "why?", but from opposite directions. 为了 looks forward: it introduces the goal you want to reach, and the main clause says what you do to reach it (为了…，我…). 因为 looks back: it gives the cause that already exists, and the result usually follows with 所以. A quick test: if you can say "in order to" in English, use 为了; if you can say "because", use 因为. 为了 can also come after 是 at the end to stress the purpose (我学地理是为了…). Learners often use 为了 for a cause, such as bad weather, which nobody wants to achieve.

| Chinese | Pinyin | English |
|---|---|---|
| 为了多看看中国的风景，他们这次不坐飞机，坐火车。 | Wèile duō kànkan Zhōngguó de fēngjǐng, tāmen zhè cì bú zuò fēijī, zuò huǒchē. | To see more of China's scenery, they're taking the train this time instead of flying. |
| 因为坐船太慢，所以我们最后还是坐飞机去了。 | Yīnwèi zuò chuán tài màn, suǒyǐ wǒmen zuìhòu háishi zuò fēijī qù le. | Because the boat was too slow, in the end we flew. |
| 我学地理是为了以后当导游。 | Wǒ xué dìlǐ shì wèile yǐhòu dāng dǎoyóu. | I'm studying geography so that I can become a tour guide later. |

**Watch out:** a cause is not a goal: ✗ 为了下雪，我们没出去 should be 因为下雪，我们没出去.

### W2 · 理解 and 了解 (to understand; to know about)
Both are verbs, but they cover different kinds of understanding. 理解 is about grasping the meaning of something said or written, or about understanding a person's reasons and feelings, often with sympathy (我很理解你). 了解 is about having solid, detailed knowledge of something: a place, a situation, a person's background. It often takes degree words (很了解, 比较了解, 不太了解) and the frame 对…很了解. It can also mean "to find out about" (我想了解一下). Use 了解 for geography and facts, 理解 for meanings and motives.

| Chinese | Pinyin | English |
|---|---|---|
| 他在上海住过五年，对上海很了解。 | Tā zài Shànghǎi zhùguo wǔ nián, duì Shànghǎi hěn liǎojiě. | He lived in Shanghai for five years, so he knows it very well. |
| 这句话的意思我不太理解。 | Zhè jù huà de yìsi wǒ bú tài lǐjiě. | I don't quite understand what this sentence means. |
| 他为什么不想回家过节，我很理解。 | Tā wèishénme bù xiǎng huí jiā guòjié, wǒ hěn lǐjiě. | I completely understand why he doesn't want to go home for the holiday. |

**Watch out:** knowledge of a place is 了解: ✗ 我很理解北京的地理 should be 我很了解北京的地理.

### W3 · 一下子 (all of a sudden, in an instant)
一下子 is an adverb that goes before the verb or adjective, never after it. It stresses that something happens very fast or with almost no time passing: a sudden change, a quick discovery, something used up in no time. It very often comes with 就 (一下子就…) and with 了 at the end, because the change is complete. In this sense it means the same as 一下 before a verb, but do not confuse it with 一下 after a verb (看一下), which softens a request.

| Chinese | Pinyin | English |
|---|---|---|
| 他太饿了，一下子就吃完了两碗面。 | Tā tài è le, yíxiàzi jiù chīwánle liǎng wǎn miàn. | He was so hungry he finished two bowls of noodles in no time. |
| 在地图上，我一下子就找到了云南。 | Zài dìtú shang, wǒ yíxiàzi jiù zhǎodàole Yúnnán. | I found Yunnan on the map straight away. |
| 一放假，学校里的人一下子就少了。 | Yí fàngjià, xuéxiào li de rén yíxiàzi jiù shǎo le. | As soon as the holidays began, the campus suddenly emptied out. |

**Watch out:** 一下子 comes before the verb: ✗ 他吃完了一下子 should be 他一下子就吃完了.

### W4 · 大多 (mostly, for the most part)
大多 is an adverb: it stands after the subject and before the verb or verb phrase, often together with 都 or 是, and says that most members of a group do something or are something. The subject names the whole group (我的同学, 这儿的饭馆), and 大多 says "most of them". Because it is an adverb, it cannot stand directly in front of a noun. To say "most people" with a noun, use 大多数 (dàduōshù) or 大部分 (dà bùfen) instead.

| Chinese | Pinyin | English |
|---|---|---|
| 我的同学寒假大多都回家了。 | Wǒ de tóngxué hánjià dàduō dōu huí jiā le. | Most of my classmates went home for winter break. |
| 这个省的人大多住在南部。 | Zhège shěng de rén dàduō zhù zài nánbù. | Most people in this province live in the south. |
| 这儿的饭馆大多很便宜。 | Zhèr de fànguǎn dàduō hěn piányi. | Most of the restaurants here are cheap. |

**Watch out:** 大多 cannot go before a noun: ✗ 大多人喜欢旅游 should be 大多数人喜欢旅游.

### W5 · 呢 (pause after a topic)
Here 呢 is not a question word. Placed after a subject or topic, it makes a short pause, as if saying "as for…", and gets the listener ready for the comment. It is most common when you list two or more parallel topics and give each its own contrasting comment: the north this, the south that; my brother this, me that. In writing, a comma follows 呢, and the parts are often separated by semicolons. Each topic needs its own comment, so the pattern is ideal for comparing regions, seasons or people's choices.

| Chinese | Pinyin | English |
|---|---|---|
| 我哥哥呢，喜欢坐飞机；我呢，喜欢坐火车。 | Wǒ gēge ne, xǐhuan zuò fēijī; wǒ ne, xǐhuan zuò huǒchē. | My brother likes flying, while I prefer the train. |
| 夏天呢，可以去哈尔滨；冬天呢，最好去云南。 | Xiàtiān ne, kěyǐ qù Hā'ěrbīn; dōngtiān ne, zuìhǎo qù Yúnnán. | In summer you can go to Harbin; in winter, Yunnan is the best choice. |
| 东部呢，人口很多；西部呢，人口比较少。 | Dōngbù ne, rénkǒu hěn duō; xībù ne, rénkǒu bǐjiào shǎo. | The east is densely populated; the west, by comparison, has few people. |

**Watch out:** pause 呢 ends a topic, not a sentence, so a comment must follow: ✗ 东部呢。 should be 东部呢，人口很多.

### Dialogue 2 · Yunnan it is
*Setting: A few days later, 林月 and 马克 video-call 陈乐 in Beijing to ask where they should go after the family holiday.*

## Exercises

### E1 · Choose the right word {type=choice}
1. 快过节了，商店里的人一下子多___了。 [起来 | 下来 | 过去] → 起来
2. 电影一开始，大家就安静___了。 [起来 | 过来 | 下来] → 下来
3. 长江在南边，___黄河在北边。 [和 | 而 | 就] → 而
4. 想看冰灯，冬天去哈尔滨最好___了。 [不过 | 不了 | 不行] → 不过
5. 我以前去___新疆，所以知道那儿冬天有多冷。 [着 | 得 | 过] → 过
6. ___多了解少数民族的生活，她在云南住了一年。 [为了 | 因为 | 所以] → 为了
7. 你说的话我不太___，你能再说一遍吗？ [了解 | 理解 | 知道] → 理解
8. 中国的河流___是从西往东流的。 [到处 | 一下子 | 大多] → 大多

### E2 · Put the words in order {type=order}
1. 天气 / 冷起来了 / 哈尔滨的 / 慢慢 → 哈尔滨的天气慢慢冷起来了。
2. 是那个省的 / 这个省的 / 两倍 / 人口 → 这个省的人口是那个省的两倍。
3. 云南旅游 / 最合适 / 冬天去 / 不过了 → 冬天去云南旅游最合适不过了。
4. 都 / 到处 / 是人 / 景点里 → 景点里到处都是人。
5. 寒假 / 一下子就 / 过完了 / 三个星期的 → 三个星期的寒假一下子就过完了。
6. 大多 / 旅游的人 / 是学生 / 来这儿 → 来这儿旅游的人大多是学生。
7. 不多 / 去过新疆的人 / 我们班 → 我们班去过新疆的人不多。

### E3 · Translate into Chinese {type=translate}
1. Most of China's big cities are in the east. → 中国的大城市大多在东部。
2. Harbin is in the northeast of China, while Guangzhou is in the south. → 哈尔滨在中国的东北，而广州在中国的南部。
3. Guangzhou and Shenzhen are both in the south of China, not far from the sea. → 广州和深圳都在中国南部，离海不远。
4. As soon as winter break started, the campus suddenly went quiet. → 一放寒假，学校里一下子就安静下来了。
5. If you want to see the scenery along the Yangtze, nothing could be better than taking a boat. → 想看长江的风景，坐船最好不过了。
6. During the holidays the tourist spots are packed with people everywhere. → 过节的时候，景点到处都是人，人山人海。
7. I've been to Tianjin; from Beijing it's only half an hour by train. → 我去过天津，从北京坐火车去只要半个小时。
8. My hometown is Nanjing, on the Yangtze River. → 我的家乡是南京，在长江边上。

### E4 · Match the word {type=match}
1. 地理 → geography
2. 家乡 → hometown
3. 路线 → route, itinerary
4. 河流 → river
5. 高原 → plateau
6. 沙漠 → desert
7. 面积 → area (of land)
8. 景点 → scenic spot, tourist spot
