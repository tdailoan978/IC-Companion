# Lesson 8 · 打工 · Working Part-Time

## Objectives
- After this lesson you can explain how students pay for their education: family savings, scholarships, loans and part-time work.
- After this lesson you can say whether you work part-time and give your reasons, linking an action to its purpose with 来.
- After this lesson you can name common student jobs such as tutoring, walking dogs and feeding cats, and talk about wages and pocket money.
- After this lesson you can make a point with a rhetorical question and describe someone's predictable habits with 不是X，就是Y.
- After this lesson you can react to news with 难怪 and 多…啊, and pick up a topic with 说到.

## Grammar

### G1 · Directional complements (III): 下来 and 出来 for results
**Pattern:** `V + 下来` (off, detached; kept, fixed) · `V + 出来` (out into the open; from nothing to something)

You know directional complements as words that show which way something moves: 跑出来 is "run out (towards me)". Many of them also have a second, non-literal use, where they describe the result of the action rather than its direction. 下来 often means that something comes off or is separated from something else (take a picture off the wall), and by extension that something is kept or fixed so it does not get lost (write it down, save money up). 出来 means that something hidden comes into the open, or that something that did not exist now does: you work out an idea, make out who someone is, see through a trick. 上, 下去, 过, 起来 and 开 have such uses too. The meaning depends on the verb, so learn each verb together with its complement. A typical slip is to use 想起来, which means "to remember", when you mean 想出来, "to come up with".

| Chinese | Pinyin | English |
|---|---|---|
| 你把墙上那张照片拿下来吧。 | Nǐ bǎ qiáng shang nà zhāng zhàopiàn ná xialai ba. | Take that photo down off the wall. |
| 老师说的话，你最好写下来。 | Lǎoshī shuō de huà, nǐ zuìhǎo xiě xialai. | You'd better write down what the teacher says. |
| 我想了一个晚上，终于想出来一个好办法。 | Wǒ xiǎngle yí ge wǎnshang, zhōngyú xiǎng chulai yí ge hǎo bànfǎ. | I thought about it all evening and finally came up with a good idea. |
| 她戴着帽子，我一下子没认出来。 | Tā dàizhe màozi, wǒ yíxiàzi méi rèn chulai. | She was wearing a hat, so I didn't recognise her at first. |

**Watch out:** for a new idea or solution use 出来, not 起来: ✗ 我想起来一个新办法 should be 我想出来一个新办法 (想起来 means "to remember").

### G2 · Connecting two verb phrases with 来 (in order to)
**Pattern:** `VP1 (the means) + 来 + VP2 (the purpose)`

Here 来 has nothing to do with "coming". Placed between two verb phrases, it says that the first action is done in order to achieve the second: 打工来减轻负担, "work in order to ease the burden". The first phrase is the means or method, often with 用 or 靠 (kào, to rely on), and the second is the goal. The order cannot be reversed: method first, aim second. Compared with 为了…, which names the purpose explicitly (为了减轻负担，我去打工 or 我打工是为了减轻负担), VP1 + 来 + VP2 keeps the action in the spotlight and sounds natural in both speech and writing. Learners tend to drop 来, which leaves two verbs side by side with an unclear relation, or to read 来 as a real movement towards the speaker.

| Chinese | Pinyin | English |
|---|---|---|
| 很多学生打工来减轻父母的负担。 | Hěn duō xuésheng dǎgōng lái jiǎnqīng fùmǔ de fùdān. | Many students work part-time to ease their parents' burden. |
| 她用奖学金来交一部分学费。 | Tā yòng jiǎngxuéjīn lái jiāo yí bùfen xuéfèi. | She uses her scholarship to pay part of her tuition. |
| 你可以多看中文电影来提高听力。 | Nǐ kěyǐ duō kàn Zhōngwén diànyǐng lái tígāo tīnglì. | You can watch more Chinese films to improve your listening. |
| 他每天早上遛狗来运动运动。 | Tā měitiān zǎoshang liù gǒu lái yùndòng yùndòng. | He walks the dog every morning to get some exercise. |

**Watch out:** the method comes before 来 and the goal after it: ✗ 他减轻父母的负担来打工 should be 他打工来减轻父母的负担.

### G3 · The particle 了 (V): background and foreground
**Pattern:** background event: `V` (no 了) · main event that moves the story on: `V + 了`

You have learned that 了 marks an action that has taken place. That does not mean every past action needs one. When you tell a story, some events only set the scene: what people usually did, what was going on, what had been arranged. These background verbs stay bare, even if they are clearly in the past. 了 is kept for the event that actually moves the story forward, the "and then this happened" moment. What is new at this level is this storytelling view of 了: a past time word alone does not call for it. Habits in the past (每天, 常常) almost never take 了. The typical learner error is to attach 了 to every past verb, which makes a story sound like a list of separate news items.

| Chinese | Pinyin | English |
|---|---|---|
| 去年夏天我在一个家庭做家教，每天下午教两个孩子英文。 | Qùnián xiàtiān wǒ zài yí ge jiātíng zuò jiājiào, měitiān xiàwǔ jiāo liǎng ge háizi Yīngwén. | Last summer I worked as a tutor for a family, teaching two children English every afternoon. |
| 那天我去银行存钱，在门口看见了我的中文老师。 | Nà tiān wǒ qù yínháng cún qián, zài ménkǒu kànjiànle wǒ de Zhōngwén lǎoshī. | That day I went to the bank to deposit some money, and at the door I ran into my Chinese teacher. |
| 他小时候住在农村，常常帮爷爷喂鸡。 | Tā xiǎo shíhou zhù zài nóngcūn, chángcháng bāng yéye wèi jī. | As a child he lived in the countryside and often helped his grandfather feed the chickens. |
| 我们正在聊打工的事，安娜走进来了。 | Wǒmen zhèngzài liáo dǎgōng de shì, Ānnà zǒu jinlai le. | We were talking about part-time jobs when Anna walked in. |

**Watch out:** a past habit takes no 了: ✗ 小时候我常常去了爷爷家 should be 小时候我常常去爷爷家.

### G4 · Rhetorical questions
**Pattern:** positive question form = strong "no" (`怎么能…呢？` `谁会…？`) · negative question form = strong "yes" (`不是…吗？` `谁不…？`)

A rhetorical question looks like a question but does not want an answer: it makes a point more forcefully than a plain statement. The rule of thumb is that it means the opposite of its form. A positive form asserts a negative: 这么低的工资，谁会做？, "who would do it?", means "nobody would", and 我怎么能告诉他？ means "I can't possibly tell him". A negative form asserts a positive: 你不是说你没钱吗？ means "you did say you had no money", and 谁不想多挣点儿钱？ means "everybody wants to earn more". You met 不是…吗 in Level 2 as a way to check a fact; used rhetorically it often sounds like a reproach. Questions with 怎么 or 哪儿 usually end in 呢. Learners sometimes answer a rhetorical question literally, or end a 怎么 question with 吗.

| Chinese | Pinyin | English |
|---|---|---|
| 工资这么低，谁会愿意做呢？ | Gōngzī zhème dī, shéi huì yuànyì zuò ne? | With pay this low, who would want to do it? |
| 谁不想有一个好工作？ | Shéi bù xiǎng yǒu yí ge hǎo gōngzuò? | Who doesn't want a good job? |
| 你不是说你没有零用钱了吗？怎么又买新衣服了？ | Nǐ bú shì shuō nǐ méiyǒu língyòngqián le ma? Zěnme yòu mǎi xīn yīfu le? | Didn't you say you'd run out of pocket money? How come you've bought new clothes again? |
| 功课这么多，我哪儿有时间去打工呢？ | Gōngkè zhème duō, wǒ nǎr yǒu shíjiān qù dǎgōng ne? | With so much homework, where would I find the time to work? |

**Watch out:** a rhetorical 怎么 or 哪儿 question ends in 呢, not 吗: ✗ 我怎么能不去吗？ should be 我怎么能不去呢？

## Words & Phrases

### W1 · 受到 (to receive)
受到 is a verb plus complement meaning "to receive" or "to get", but only for abstract things that happen to you: a welcome, an education, praise, criticism, someone's influence. The object is usually a noun with a modifier in front, such as 良好的教育 or 大家的欢迎. Do not mix it up with 收到 (shōudào), which sounds almost the same and takes concrete things you physically get, like a letter, a parcel or a gift. Listen for the tone of the first syllable: 受 is fourth tone, 收 is first tone.

| Chinese | Pinyin | English |
|---|---|---|
| 她从小就受到了良好的教育。 | Tā cóngxiǎo jiù shòudàole liánghǎo de jiàoyù. | She received a good education from an early age. |
| 新来的老师受到了学生们的欢迎。 | Xīn lái de lǎoshī shòudàole xuéshengmen de huānyíng. | The new teacher has been well received by the students. |
| 他小时候受到了爷爷很大的影响。 | Tā xiǎo shíhou shòudàole yéye hěn dà de yǐngxiǎng. | As a child he was strongly influenced by his grandfather. |

**Watch out:** concrete things take 收到: ✗ 我受到了妈妈的信 should be 我收到了妈妈的信.

### W2 · 压力 (pressure)
压力 is a noun for pressure, usually the mental kind: from exams, money, work or other people's expectations. The basic patterns are 有压力 or 压力很大 ("to be under pressure"), 给 someone 压力 ("to put pressure on someone"), and X 对 Y 的压力 ("the pressure X puts on Y"). It combines naturally with 减轻 ("to ease"). To say where the pressure comes from, use 学习压力, 工作压力 or 经济 (jīngjì) 压力. 压力 is a noun, so it is never used as a verb meaning "to press someone".

| Chinese | Pinyin | English |
|---|---|---|
| 快考试了，大家的压力都很大。 | Kuài kǎoshì le, dàjiā de yālì dōu hěn dà. | Exams are coming up, and everyone is under a lot of pressure. |
| 爸爸妈妈从来不给我压力。 | Bàba māma cónglái bù gěi wǒ yālì. | My parents never put pressure on me. |
| 房租太贵，对我们的压力不小。 | Fángzū tài guì, duì wǒmen de yālì bù xiǎo. | The rent is very high, and that's quite a strain on us. |

**Watch out:** 压力 is not a verb: ✗ 他压力我 should be 他给我压力.

### W3 · 减轻 (to lessen)
减轻 is a verb meaning "to make lighter, to reduce". It is normally followed by an abstract noun that weighs on someone: 负担, 压力 or 头疼 (headache). It can also come after the noun as its predicate, often with 了 to say that the weight has become lighter (压力减轻了). It is the natural verb for helping people out: children work to ease their parents' burden, a friend takes some tasks to lighten yours. For prices or numbers going down, Chinese uses other verbs, so do not stretch 减轻 to "the price got lower".

| Chinese | Pinyin | English |
|---|---|---|
| 我打工是为了减轻爸爸妈妈的负担。 | Wǒ dǎgōng shì wèile jiǎnqīng bàba māma de fùdān. | I work part-time to ease my parents' burden. |
| 考完试以后，我的压力减轻了很多。 | Kǎowán shì yǐhòu, wǒ de yālì jiǎnqīngle hěn duō. | Once the exams were over, my stress eased a lot. |
| 吃了这种药，头疼会减轻一点儿。 | Chīle zhè zhǒng yào, tóuténg huì jiǎnqīng yìdiǎnr. | This medicine will ease your headache a little. |

### W4 · 适合 and 合适 (to suit and suitable)
These two share their characters but not their grammar. 适合 is a transitive verb: it needs an object, the person or thing that is suited (这个工作适合你). It can also start a pivotal sentence, where the object is also the subject of what follows (这个地方适合学生住). 合适, which you learned earlier, is an adjective: it is the predicate itself (这个时间很合适) or describes a noun with 的 (合适的工作). Because 合适 is an adjective, it cannot take an object; to say for whom something is suitable, use 对 (对我很合适). Both can appear in one sentence: 合适 for the verdict, 适合 for the target.

| Chinese | Pinyin | English |
|---|---|---|
| 这个工作时间不长，很适合学生。 | Zhège gōngzuò shíjiān bù cháng, hěn shìhé xuésheng. | This job doesn't take many hours, so it suits students well. |
| 你帮我看看，这件衣服合适不合适？ | Nǐ bāng wǒ kànkan, zhè jiàn yīfu héshì bu héshì? | Have a look for me: does this piece of clothing fit all right? |
| 我觉得这儿很合适，适合我们一家人住。 | Wǒ juéde zhèr hěn héshì, shìhé wǒmen yì jiā rén zhù. | I think this place is just right; it suits our family. |

**Watch out:** 合适 takes no object: ✗ 这个工作很合适你 should be 这个工作很适合你.

### W5 · 影响 (to influence, to affect; influence)
影响 works both as a verb and as a noun. As a verb it takes a direct object: 影响学习 ("to affect one's studies"), 影响身体, 影响别人 ("to disturb others"). Often the effect is a bad one, so 别影响我 means "don't distract me". As a noun it appears most often in the pattern X 对 Y 有影响 ("X has an effect on Y"), with 很大的, 不好的 or 没有 to say how much. Context and word order tell you which part of speech is meant: right after a subject and before an object it is the verb; after 有 or 的 it is the noun.

| Chinese | Pinyin | English |
|---|---|---|
| 晚上睡得太少会影响第二天上课。 | Wǎnshang shuì de tài shǎo huì yǐngxiǎng dì èr tiān shàngkè. | Too little sleep at night will affect your classes the next day. |
| 你在这儿打电话，会影响别人看书。 | Nǐ zài zhèr dǎ diànhuà, huì yǐngxiǎng biérén kàn shū. | If you make phone calls here, you'll disturb other people who are reading. |
| 我奶奶对我的影响很大。 | Wǒ nǎinai duì wǒ de yǐngxiǎng hěn dà. | My grandmother has had a big influence on me. |

**Watch out:** the noun pattern puts 对 first: ✗ 打工有影响对学习 should be 打工对学习有影响.

### W6 · 取得 (to obtain)
取得 is a verb meaning "to obtain, to gain", and it is used with abstract results that come from effort: 经验, 好成绩 (good results), 进步 (progress), or an agreement. It sounds a little more formal than 得到, and it is not used for physical objects you simply pick up or buy. A typical frame is 在…中/方面取得…, but in everyday speech it usually follows the subject directly. The opposite case, getting a thing such as a book or a ticket, uses 拿, 买 or 得到.

| Chinese | Pinyin | English |
|---|---|---|
| 打工可以取得很多工作经验。 | Dǎgōng kěyǐ qǔdé hěn duō gōngzuò jīngyàn. | Working part-time can give you a lot of work experience. |
| 这个学期他学中文取得了很大的进步。 | Zhège xuéqī tā xué Zhōngwén qǔdéle hěn dà de jìnbù. | This semester he made great progress in his Chinese. |
| 她每门课都取得了好成绩。 | Tā měi mén kè dōu qǔdéle hǎo chéngjì. | She got good grades in every class. |

**Watch out:** 取得 is not for objects: ✗ 我在图书馆取得了一本书 should be 我在图书馆借了一本书.

### W7 · 说到 (speaking of)
说到 introduces a further remark on something that has just come up in the conversation. You can use it to carry on with your own point or to pick up what the other person said and take it somewhere new. The topic comes right after 说到, often closed by a comma, and then the comment follows: 说到打工，我也想找个工作。 It is spoken and friendly, a little like English "speaking of…" or "talking of…". Unlike 关于 (about), which only marks a topic, 说到 always links back to what was just said.

| Chinese | Pinyin | English |
|---|---|---|
| 说到吃饭，我知道一家新开的饭馆。 | Shuōdào chīfàn, wǒ zhīdao yì jiā xīn kāi de fànguǎn. | Speaking of eating, I know a restaurant that's just opened. |
| 说到零用钱，我小时候每个星期只有五块。 | Shuōdào língyòngqián, wǒ xiǎo shíhou měi ge xīngqī zhǐ yǒu wǔ kuài. | Talking of pocket money, when I was little I only got five dollars a week. |
| 说到奖学金，我想起来了，我还没告诉妈妈我拿到奖学金了。 | Shuōdào jiǎngxuéjīn, wǒ xiǎng qilai le, wǒ hái méi gàosu māma wǒ nádào jiǎngxuéjīn le. | Speaking of scholarships, that reminds me: I still haven't told my mum I got one. |

### W8 · 不是X，就是Y (either X or Y)
This pattern lists two alternatives and says that, between them, they cover the situation: "if it isn't X, it's Y". It is very common for describing habits and routines that are easy to predict, often with a slightly teasing or complaining tone (他周末不是睡觉，就是上网). X and Y can be verb phrases, nouns or whole clauses, but they should be parallel in form. 不是 here is pronounced bú shì, and the two halves can be said without a pause in fast speech. Compare 或者 (or), which only offers a choice; 不是X，就是Y claims that nothing else happens.

| Chinese | Pinyin | English |
|---|---|---|
| 马克周末不是在饭馆吃饭，就是在家里看电视。 | Mǎkè zhōumò bú shì zài fànguǎn chīfàn, jiù shì zài jiā li kàn diànshì. | At the weekend Mark is either eating out or watching TV at home. |
| 这几天不是刮风，就是下雨。 | Zhè jǐ tiān bú shì guāfēng, jiù shì xiàyǔ. | These past few days it's been either windy or rainy. |
| 他一拿到零用钱，不是买吃的，就是买衣服。 | Tā yì nádào língyòngqián, bú shì mǎi chī de, jiù shì mǎi yīfu. | As soon as he gets his pocket money, he spends it on either food or clothes. |

**Watch out:** the second half needs 就是, not 也是: ✗ 他不是在图书馆，也是在宿舍 should be 他不是在图书馆，就是在宿舍.

### W9 · 难怪 (no wonder)
难怪 is an adverb that works almost like an interjection. You say it when a fact that seemed strange suddenly makes sense because you have learned the reason. It usually opens the sentence, followed by the fact that is no longer surprising: 难怪你这么累. The explanation can come before it, or after it, often introduced by 原来 ("so that's why"). Because it reacts to new information, it is common in conversation, right after the other person has explained something. It can also stand alone as a reply: 难怪！

| Chinese | Pinyin | English |
|---|---|---|
| 你昨天晚上打工到十二点？难怪你上课的时候想睡觉。 | Nǐ zuótiān wǎnshang dǎgōng dào shí'èr diǎn? Nánguài nǐ shàngkè de shíhou xiǎng shuìjiào. | You worked until midnight last night? No wonder you were sleepy in class. |
| 难怪他这几天这么高兴，原来他拿到奖学金了。 | Nánguài tā zhè jǐ tiān zhème gāoxìng, yuánlái tā nádào jiǎngxuéjīn le. | No wonder he's been so happy these days: he got a scholarship. |
| 她以前在中国住过三年，难怪中文说得那么好。 | Tā yǐqián zài Zhōngguó zhùguo sān nián, nánguài Zhōngwén shuō de nàme hǎo. | She lived in China for three years before, so no wonder her Chinese is so good. |

**Watch out:** 难怪 goes before the surprising fact, not before the reason: ✗ 难怪他拿到奖学金了，他很高兴 should be 难怪他很高兴，原来他拿到奖学金了.

### W10 · 多 (how ... it is)
In an exclamation, 多 placed before an adjective or a verb phrase of feeling means "how" or "so": 多好啊！ "how wonderful!" It expresses the speaker's strong feeling and is not a question, even though 多 also appears in questions like 多大. The sentence usually ends in 啊 (or 呀 after a vowel), and 多么 (duōme) is a slightly more emphatic or written form. Typical uses are admiring something, complaining, or imagining: 要是我也有一只狗，多好啊！ Do not add 很 after 多, since 多 already does the intensifying.

| Chinese | Pinyin | English |
|---|---|---|

## Exercises

### E1 · Choose the right word {type=choice}
1. 我每天走路去上课___少花一点儿钱。 [再 | 来 | 的] → 来
2. 这个电影在中国___了学生们的欢迎。 [收到 | 受到 | 交到] → 受到
3. 你这么喜欢孩子，做家教很___你。 [合适 | 取得 | 适合] → 适合
4. 下午三点对我不太___，我们四点见吧。 [合适 | 难怪 | 乖] → 合适
5. 这个月马克不是遛狗，___喂猫，每天都很忙。 [也是 | 就是 | 还是] → 就是
6. 功课这么多，我怎么能不着急___？ [吧 | 吗 | 呢] → 呢
7. ___马克最近每天都去公园，原来他找了一个遛狗的工作。 [难怪 | 虽然 | 因为] → 难怪
8. 你说的这个办法很好，是谁想___的？ [下来 | 出来 | 过去] → 出来

### E2 · Put the words in order {type=order}
1. 马克 / 来 / 还林月的钱 / 用遛狗的工资 → 马克用遛狗的工资来还林月的钱。
2. 太多 / 会 / 打工 / 影响学习 → 打工太多会影响学习。
3. 银行 / 我还 / 两百块钱 / 欠 → 我还欠银行两百块钱。
4. 难怪 / 这么 / 他今天 / 高兴 → 难怪他今天这么高兴。
5. 都 / 存下来了 / 他把 / 这个月的工资 → 他把这个月的工资都存下来了。
6. 啊 / 这只猫 / 乖 / 多 → 这只猫多乖啊！
7. 不太合适 / 这个时间 / 对我 → 这个时间对我不太合适。

### E3 · Translate into Chinese {type=translate}
1. I work part-time to ease my parents' burden. → 我打工来减轻父母的负担。
2. My scholarship isn't enough to pay my tuition, so I also have a loan. → 我的奖学金不够交学费，所以我还有贷款。
3. Who doesn't want more pocket money? → 谁不想要更多的零用钱？
4. On weekends he is either sleeping or watching TV. → 他周末不是睡觉，就是看电视。
5. No wonder you're so tired: you walked five dogs today! → 难怪你这么累，原来你今天遛了五只狗！
6. Does working part-time affect your studies? → 打工对你的学习有影响吗？
7. I couldn't make out who she was. → 我没看出来她是谁。
8. My parents' income isn't high, so I want to find a part-time job. → 我爸爸妈妈的收入不高，所以我想去打工。

### E4 · Match the word {type=match}
1. 压力 → pressure
2. 负担 → burden
3. 工资 → wages, pay
4. 零用钱 → pocket money
5. 奖学金 → scholarship money
6. 银行 → bank
7. 农村 → countryside
8. 欠 → to owe
