# Lesson 2 · 宿舍生活 · Dorm Life

## Objectives
- After this lesson you can name the basic furniture and things in a dorm room and say where each one stands or hangs, putting the place first: 窗户下面摆着两张书桌.
- After this lesson you can describe where you live, from the building and the floor to the laundry machines and the shops nearby.
- After this lesson you can comment on someone's room or food, softening your opinion with 比较 or making it strong with 得很.
- After this lesson you can disagree tactfully with 恐怕 and 不怎么样, and talk about what you usually do with 一般.
- After this lesson you can link a reason and a result with or without 因为 and 所以.

## Grammar

### G1 · Existential sentences: what is where
**Pattern:** `Place + V (+ 着 / 了) + number + measure + noun`

When you describe a room, Chinese starts with the place and ends with the thing that is there: "under the window stand two desks", not "two desks are under the window". The place word is the subject and takes no 在. The thing comes last, usually with a number and a measure word, because it is new information. Only a few verbs fit the slot: 有 and 是, and verbs that say how something is placed or held, such as 放, 摆, 挂, 站, 坐 and 住, normally with 着. 有 and 是 are not the same here: 有 just says that something is there, and other things may be there too, while 是 says that the place is filled by this one thing or one kind of thing. English speakers tend to start with the thing, or with 有, which does not work in Chinese.

| Chinese | Pinyin | English |
|---|---|---|
| 我的书桌上放着一台电脑和几本书。 | Wǒ de shūzhuō shang fàngzhe yì tái diànnǎo hé jǐ běn shū. | On my desk there's a computer and a few books. |
| 衣柜旁边摆着两把椅子。 | Yīguì pángbiān bǎizhe liǎng bǎ yǐzi. | Two chairs stand next to the wardrobe. |
| 宿舍楼前边有一个餐厅，还有一家小书店。 | Sùshè lóu qiánbian yǒu yí ge cāntīng, hái yǒu yì jiā xiǎo shūdiàn. | In front of the dorm there's a cafeteria, and a small bookshop too. |
| 学校东边是一条大马路。 | Xuéxiào dōngbian shì yì tiáo dà mǎlù. | East of the school is a big road. |

**Watch out:** do not start with 有 and end with the place: ✗ 有一张床在房间里 should be 房间里有一张床.

### G2 · 比较: rather, relatively
**Pattern:** `Subj + 比较 + Adj / verb of feeling`

You met 比较 in Lesson 1. As an adverb it means "rather" or "fairly": it gives a loose, general degree, without saying what the thing is being measured against. That makes it ideal for mild opinions and gentle complaints: the room is rather small, the food is fairly cheap, I rather like quiet places. It goes right before the adjective or the verb of feeling (喜欢, 怕, 想), in the slot where 很 would go. What 比较 cannot do is make an actual comparison between two named things: "my room is bigger than yours" needs 比, and "even bigger" needs 更. Many learners treat 比较 as English "more … than" and put it where 比 belongs; that sentence does not work in Chinese.

| Chinese | Pinyin | English |
|---|---|---|
| 这栋楼比较旧，可是住着很舒服。 | Zhè dòng lóu bǐjiào jiù, kěshì zhùzhe hěn shūfu. | This building is rather old, but it's comfortable to live in. |
| 我比较喜欢一个人住。 | Wǒ bǐjiào xǐhuan yí ge rén zhù. | I'd rather live on my own. |
| 今天比较冷，你多穿件衣服吧。 | Jīntiān bǐjiào lěng, nǐ duō chuān jiàn yīfu ba. | It's fairly cold today, so put on an extra layer. |

**Watch out:** 比较 does not compare two named things: ✗ 我的房间比较你的大 should be 我的房间比你的大.

### G3 · …得很: extremely
**Pattern:** `Adj / verb of feeling + 得很`

得很 goes straight after an adjective, or after a verb of thought or feeling, and pushes the degree up to "extremely" or "terribly". It is stronger than 很 + adjective, which in everyday speech often means no more than plain "good" or "busy". The judgement can be positive (干净得很, spotless) or negative (忙得很, swamped; 吵得很, terribly noisy). It is lively and colloquial, and it usually ends the clause. Do not combine it with 很 in front of the adjective: one marker of degree is enough. And remember that the adjective comes first: 得很 is a complement, not an adverb, so it cannot go before the word it intensifies.

| Chinese | Pinyin | English |
|---|---|---|
| 这个星期我忙得很，没有时间去看你。 | Zhège xīngqī wǒ máng de hěn, méiyǒu shíjiān qù kàn nǐ. | I'm swamped this week, I've got no time to come and see you. |
| 她的房间干净得很。 | Tā de fángjiān gānjìng de hěn. | Her room is spotless. |
| 考试快到了，他心里着急得很。 | Kǎoshì kuài dào le, tā xīnli zháojí de hěn. | The exam is coming up and he's extremely worried. |

**Watch out:** no 很 in front: ✗ 宿舍很热得很 should be 宿舍热得很.

### G4 · Conjunctions: leaving out 因为 and 所以
**Pattern:** `(因为) reason, (所以) result`

因为 introduces a reason and 所以 the result, and you can use them together, or either one alone. In everyday speech, though, Chinese speakers very often drop both, because the order already makes the link clear: first the reason, then what follows from it. A sentence such as 宿舍没有空调，夏天热得很 needs no conjunction at all. Adding 因为…所以… makes the logic explicit, sounds a little more careful or formal, and helps when the reason is long or surprising. The usual order is reason first, result second, the opposite of the typical English "…, because …"; a reason added afterwards with 因为 is also possible, but without conjunctions the reason must come first. When 所以 is used, it must introduce the result; learners sometimes attach it to the reason.

| Chinese | Pinyin | English |
|---|---|---|
| 因为宿舍离教室很近，所以我每天走路去上课。 | Yīnwèi sùshè lí jiàoshì hěn jìn, suǒyǐ wǒ měitiān zǒulù qù shàngkè. | Because the dorm is close to the classrooms, I walk to class every day. |
| 我明天有考试，今天不能出去玩儿。 | Wǒ míngtiān yǒu kǎoshì, jīntiān bù néng chūqu wánr. | I have an exam tomorrow, so I can't go out today. |
| 这儿的房租很便宜，所以住的学生很多。 | Zhèr de fángzū hěn piányi, suǒyǐ zhù de xuésheng hěn duō. | The rent here is cheap, so lots of students live here. |

**Watch out:** 所以 marks the result, not the reason: ✗ 我很累，所以我昨天没睡好 should be 我昨天没睡好，所以很累.

## Words & Phrases

### W1 · 恐怕 (I'm afraid, probably)
恐怕 is an adverb that gives your own guess about a situation, usually a guess you are not happy about: "I'm afraid…", "probably…". It goes before the verb or before the whole clause, and it is often softened further with 吧 at the end. Because the guess is always the speaker's, people rarely say 我恐怕; the subject in the sentence is the thing or person you are talking about, or it is left out. That makes 恐怕 a polite way to give bad news or to disagree without sounding blunt: 恐怕不行 sounds much softer than 不行.

| Chinese | Pinyin | English |
|---|---|---|
| 天这么黑，恐怕要下雨了。 | Tiān zhème hēi, kǒngpà yào xiàyǔ le. | It's so dark, I'm afraid it's going to rain. |
| 这么多作业，今天晚上恐怕写不完。 | Zhème duō zuòyè, jīntiān wǎnshang kǒngpà xiě bu wán. | With this much homework, I probably won't finish it tonight. |
| 他现在恐怕还没起床吧。 | Tā xiànzài kǒngpà hái méi qǐchuáng ba. | I suspect he isn't up yet. |

**Watch out:** do not copy English "I'm afraid he…" by putting 我 before 恐怕: ✗ 我恐怕他不来 should be 恐怕他不来了.

### W2 · 差不多 (about, roughly; almost the same)
差不多 has two jobs. As an adverb it goes before an amount, a verb or an adjective and means "about" or "almost": 差不多十分钟, 差不多每天, 差不多高. As a predicate it means "about the same", and then the two things are usually linked with 跟: A 跟 B 差不多. It is a very common, relaxed word for estimates, so you will hear it whenever people talk about time, prices, distances or sizes. When the comparison names a quality, that adjective follows 差不多 (差不多大, 差不多贵).

| Chinese | Pinyin | English |
|---|---|---|
| 从宿舍走到图书馆差不多要十分钟。 | Cóng sùshè zǒu dào túshūguǎn chàbuduō yào shí fēnzhōng. | It takes about ten minutes to walk from the dorm to the library. |
| 我跟我的同屋差不多高。 | Wǒ gēn wǒ de tóngwū chàbuduō gāo. | My roommate and I are about the same height. |
| 这两个房间差不多，你住哪个都行。 | Zhè liǎng ge fángjiān chàbuduō, nǐ zhù nǎge dōu xíng. | These two rooms are about the same, either one is fine for you. |

**Watch out:** 差不多 needs 跟 to compare two things: ✗ 我的房间差不多你的房间 should be 我的房间跟你的房间差不多.

### W3 · 吵 (noisy; to quarrel)
吵 works in two ways. As an adjective it means "noisy" and is the opposite of 安静: it is the predicate of a place or a time (楼里很吵, 晚上太吵了). As a verb it means "to make noise" or "to quarrel"; the person you argue with is introduced with 跟, and the verb often takes 起来 ("started to argue"). The short command 别吵了 can mean either "stop making noise" or "stop arguing", depending on the situation. Context and position tell the two uses apart.

| Chinese | Pinyin | English |
|---|---|---|
| 这家饭馆儿太吵了，我们换一家吧。 | Zhè jiā fànguǎnr tài chǎo le, wǒmen huàn yì jiā ba. | This restaurant is too noisy, let's go somewhere else. |
| 他们两个说着说着就吵起来了。 | Tāmen liǎng ge shuōzhe shuōzhe jiù chǎo qilai le. | The two of them were talking and ended up quarrelling. |
| 别吵了，我在学习呢！ | Bié chǎo le, wǒ zài xuéxí ne! | Stop making noise, I'm studying! |

**Watch out:** the other person comes in with 跟: ✗ 我吵我的同屋 should be 我跟我的同屋吵起来了.

### W4 · 安静 (quiet)
安静 is an adjective. It is the predicate of a place, a time or a person (图书馆里很安静), it goes before a noun with 的 (安静的地方), and with 一点儿 or 点儿 it becomes a request: 请安静一点儿. Like other adjectives it takes 得很 for emphasis. It describes the absence of noise and also a calm, quiet personality, so 她很安静 means she is a quiet person. Its opposite for places is 吵.

| Chinese | Pinyin | English |
|---|---|---|
| 我喜欢在安静的地方看书。 | Wǒ xǐhuan zài ānjìng de dìfang kàn shū. | I like to read in a quiet place. |
| 大家安静点儿，老师来了！ | Dàjiā ānjìng diǎnr, lǎoshī lái le! | Quiet, everyone, the teacher's here! |
| 我的同屋很安静，不太说话。 | Wǒ de tóngwū hěn ānjìng, bú tài shuōhuà. | My roommate is very quiet and doesn't talk much. |

**Watch out:** to say a place is "a quiet place", link it with 是: ✗ 图书馆很安静的地方 should be 图书馆是一个很安静的地方.

### W5 · 一般 (generally)
一般 is an adjective, but it is mostly used as an adverb meaning "usually" or "as a rule". It goes before the verb or before 都, after the subject or at the very start of the sentence, and it describes habits and what is normally true: 我一般七点起床, 学生一般都住在宿舍. A time word can come before or after it. As a predicate adjective 一般 means "ordinary, so-so" (这家的菜很一般), a mild way of saying something is not great.

| Chinese | Pinyin | English |
|---|---|---|
| 周末我一般在宿舍洗衣服。 | Zhōumò wǒ yìbān zài sùshè xǐ yīfu. | On weekends I usually do my laundry in the dorm. |
| 新生一般都住在学校里。 | Xīnshēng yìbān dōu zhù zài xuéxiào li. | Freshmen generally all live on campus. |
| 这家饭馆儿的菜很一般，不怎么好吃。 | Zhè jiā fànguǎnr de cài hěn yìbān, bù zěnme hǎochī. | The food at this restaurant is nothing special, not very tasty. |

**Watch out:** 一般 goes before the verb, not at the end: ✗ 我七点起床一般 should be 我一般七点起床.

### W6 · 不怎么样 (not that great, just so-so)
不怎么样 is a colloquial predicate: it follows the thing you are judging and gives a lukewarm or negative opinion, "not much good". Because it can sound blunt, it is used among friends, and speakers often add a reason afterwards to explain. To sound gentler, add 我觉得 in front or use 不太好. Do not put an adjective after it; if you want to name the quality, use 不怎么 + adjective instead (不怎么好吃, not very tasty).

| Chinese | Pinyin | English |
|---|---|---|
| 那个电影不怎么样，你别去看了。 | Nàge diànyǐng bù zěnmeyàng, nǐ bié qù kàn le. | That movie isn't much good, don't bother going. |
| 我觉得他的新公寓不怎么样，又小又旧。 | Wǒ juéde tā de xīn gōngyù bù zěnmeyàng, yòu xiǎo yòu jiù. | I don't think much of his new apartment, it's small and old. |
| 我的中文不怎么样，请你说慢一点儿。 | Wǒ de Zhōngwén bù zěnmeyàng, qǐng nǐ shuō màn yìdiǎnr. | My Chinese isn't great, please speak a bit more slowly. |

**Watch out:** nothing follows 不怎么样: ✗ 这个菜不怎么样好吃 should be 这个菜不怎么样 or 这个菜不怎么好吃.

### W7 · 地道 (authentic, genuine)
地道 is an adjective meaning "the real thing": it says that something matches the true standard of its place of origin. It is used most often for food (地道的北京菜) and for the way someone speaks (他的上海话很地道). It can be the predicate, usually with 很 or 得很, or it can describe a noun with 的. As praise for a foreigner's Chinese it is a strong compliment. In Taiwan and southern China people often say 道地 instead.

| Chinese | Pinyin | English |
|---|---|---|
| 这家餐馆儿的北京菜很地道。 | Zhè jiā cānguǎnr de Běijīng cài hěn dìdao. | The Beijing dishes at this restaurant are really authentic. |
| 她在上海住了十年，上海话说得很地道。 | Tā zài Shànghǎi zhùle shí nián, Shànghǎi huà shuō de hěn dìdao. | She lived in Shanghai for ten years and speaks Shanghainese like a local. |
| 我想找一家地道的中国饭馆儿。 | Wǒ xiǎng zhǎo yì jiā dìdao de Zhōngguó fànguǎnr. | I'd like to find an authentic Chinese restaurant. |

## Dialogues

### Dialogue 1 · Anna's new room
*Setting: On a Saturday afternoon, 林月 and 马克 visit 安娜's new dorm room on the fourth floor of an old building for the first time.*

| Speaker | Chinese | Pinyin | English |
|---|---|---|---|
| 马克 | 安娜，你住的这栋楼一共几层？看起来比较旧啊。 | Ānnà, nǐ zhù de zhè dòng lóu yígòng jǐ céng? Kàn qǐlai bǐjiào jiù a. | Anna, how many floors does your building have? It looks rather old. |
| 安娜 | 五层。是很旧，听说已经有一百年了。不过它离大马路比较远，所以晚上安静得很。 | Wǔ céng. Shì hěn jiù, tīngshuō yǐjīng yǒu yìbǎi nián le. Búguò tā lí dà mǎlù bǐjiào yuǎn, suǒyǐ wǎnshang ānjìng de hěn. | Five. It is old, apparently a hundred years old. But it's fairly far from the main road, so it's wonderfully quiet at night. |
| 林月 | 房间也不小。窗户下面摆着两张书桌，哪张是你的？ | Fángjiān yě bù xiǎo. Chuānghu xiàmian bǎizhe liǎng zhāng shūzhuō, nǎ zhāng shì nǐ de? | The room isn't small either. There are two desks under the window. Which one is yours? |
| 安娜 | 左边那张是我的，右边那张是我同屋的。她是从加州来的新生，这个周末回家了。 | Zuǒbian nà zhāng shì wǒ de, yòubian nà zhāng shì wǒ tóngwū de. Tā shì cóng Jiāzhōu lái de xīnshēng, zhège zhōumò huí jiā le. | The one on the left is mine, the one on the right is my roommate's. She's a freshman from California, and she's gone home this weekend. |
| 马克 | 床上怎么放着一条被子，还有一条毯子？现在才九月啊。 | Chuáng shang zěnme fàngzhe yì tiáo bèizi, hái yǒu yì tiáo tǎnzi? Xiànzài cái jiǔyuè a. | Why is there a comforter on the bed, and a blanket as well? It's only September. |
| 安娜 | 被子是我同屋的，她特别怕冷。我一般只用毯子。 | Bèizi shì wǒ tóngwū de, tā tèbié pà lěng. Wǒ yìbān zhǐ yòng tǎnzi. | The comforter is my roommate's, she really feels the cold. I usually just use the blanket. |
| 林月 | 门后边挂着的是什么？是你踢球穿的衣服吗？ | Mén hòubian guàzhe de shì shénme? Shì nǐ tī qiú chuān de yīfu ma? | What's that hanging behind the door? Is it what you wear for soccer? |
| 安娜 | 对。衣柜是我们两个人一起用的，太小了，放不下。那个小柜子里都是日用品和文具。 | Duì. Yīguì shì wǒmen liǎng ge rén yìqǐ yòng de, tài xiǎo le, fàng bu xià. Nàge xiǎo guìzi li dōu shì rìyòngpǐn hé wénjù. | Yes. We share the wardrobe and it's too small, there isn't room. That little cabinet is full of toiletries and stationery. |
| 马克 | 房间里有点儿热，没有空调吗？ | Fángjiān li yǒudiǎnr rè, méiyǒu kōngtiáo ma? | It's a bit hot in here. Is there no air conditioning? |
| 安娜 | 没有。楼太旧了，恐怕以后也不会有。不过夏天差不多过去了，开着窗户就行。 | Méiyǒu. Lóu tài jiù le, kǒngpà yǐhòu yě bú huì yǒu. Búguò xiàtiān chàbuduō guòqu le, kāizhe chuānghu jiù xíng. | No. The building's too old, I doubt it ever will. But summer's nearly over, and leaving the window open does the job. |
| 林月 | 洗衣服方便吗？我们楼的洗衣机常常坏。 | Xǐ yīfu fāngbiàn ma? Wǒmen lóu de xǐyījī chángcháng huài. | Is it easy to do laundry? The washing machines in our building are always breaking down. |
| 安娜 | 方便得很。楼下有五台洗衣机、五台干衣机，旁边还有一家小商店，买东西也很方便。 | Fāngbiàn de hěn. Lóuxià yǒu wǔ tái xǐyījī, wǔ tái gānyījī, pángbiān hái yǒu yì jiā xiǎo shāngdiàn, mǎi dōngxi yě hěn fāngbiàn. | Very easy. Downstairs there are five washers and five dryers, and there's a little shop next door, so shopping's easy too. |

### Dialogue 2 · Lunch at Mark's
*Setting: The next Sunday, 安娜 and 林月 come to 马克's off-campus apartment for lunch, which he insists on cooking himself.*

| Speaker | Chinese | Pinyin | English |
|---|---|---|---|
| 马克 | 请进！我的公寓比较小，你们坐吧。 | Qǐng jìn! Wǒ de gōngyù bǐjiào xiǎo, nǐmen zuò ba. | Come in! My apartment's rather small, have a seat. |

## Exercises

### E1 · Choose the right word {type=choice}
1. 门上___着一张我家人的照片。 [有 | 挂 | 是] → 挂
2. 这个菜好吃___，你多吃点儿。 [很 | 比较 | 得很] → 得很
3. 今天___昨天冷，你多穿点儿衣服。 [比 | 比较 | 更] → 比
4. 外边下雨了，他___不来了吧。 [一般 | 恐怕 | 差不多] → 恐怕
5. 安娜的房间跟我的___，也摆着两张书桌。 [比较 | 一般 | 差不多] → 差不多
6. 别___了，我在看书呢！ [吵 | 安静 | 地道] → 吵
7. 星期六我___睡到十点才起床。 [比较 | 一般 | 地道] → 一般
8. 我在北京住过，这家餐馆儿的北京菜很___，跟我在北京吃的一样。 [安静 | 着急 | 地道] → 地道

### E2 · Put the words in order {type=order}
1. 门后边 / 两件衣服 / 宿舍的 / 挂着 → 宿舍的门后边挂着两件衣服。
2. 一张 / 窗户下面 / 书桌 / 摆着 → 窗户下面摆着一张书桌。
3. 有 / 洗衣机 / 我们楼下 / 三台 → 我们楼下有三台洗衣机。
4. 得很 / 这个图书馆 / 安静 / 里边 → 这个图书馆里边安静得很。
5. 差不多 / 跟你的 / 我的房间 / 大 → 我的房间跟你的差不多大。
6. 吃午饭 / 都 / 我们一般 / 在餐厅 → 我们一般都在餐厅吃午饭。
7. 夏天热得很 / 所以 / 因为宿舍 / 没有空调 → 因为宿舍没有空调，所以夏天热得很。

### E3 · Translate into Chinese {type=translate}
1. There are two desks and two beds in my dorm room. → 我的宿舍里有两张书桌、两张床。
2. Two of my roommate's shirts are hanging behind the door. → 门后边挂着我同屋的两件衬衫。
3. This building is very old; I'm afraid it doesn't have air conditioning. → 这栋楼很旧，恐怕没有空调。
4. The cafeteria food is just so-so. → 餐厅的菜不怎么样。
5. On weekends I usually do my laundry. → 周末我一般洗衣服。
6. It's fairly quiet here, because it's far from the road. → 这儿离马路远，所以比较安静。
7. Don't worry, we'll go to that restaurant together in a few days. → 别着急，过几天我们一起去那家餐馆儿。

### E4 · Match the word {type=match}
1. 同屋 → roommate

3. 毯子 → blanket
4. 衣柜 → wardrobe
5. 日用品 → daily necessities
