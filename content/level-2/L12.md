# Lesson 12 · 吃饭 · Dining

## Objectives
- After this lesson you can ask for a table, order dishes and drinks with 来, and say how you want them cooked.
- After this lesson you can describe tastes such as sour, sweet and spicy, and make them vivid by doubling the adjective.
- After this lesson you can say that an action reached a result (finished, ready, wrong, clear) with a resultative complement.
- After this lesson you can stress "not even one" or "not at all" with 一…也/都…不/没.
- After this lesson you can tell 刚 from 刚才 and ask for more or less of something with 多/少 + verb.

## Grammar

### G1 · 一…也/都…不/没… (not even one)
**Pattern:** `(Subject +) 一 + Measure word + Noun + 也 / 都 + 不 / 没 + Verb` and `一点儿 + 也 / 都 + 不 + Adj`

This is the emphatic way to say "none at all". You name the smallest possible amount (one seat, one cup, a tiny bit) and then deny even that. The amount phrase must come before 也 or 都, and those come before 不 or 没, so the thing being counted ends up in front of the verb, the opposite of English order. With adjectives, use 一点儿也不 for "not at all". Learners usually leave the object after the verb, where it would sit in an ordinary sentence.

| Chinese | Pinyin | English |
|---|---|---|
| 饭馆里一个位子都没有。 | Fànguǎn li yí ge wèizi dōu méiyǒu. | There isn't a single seat in the restaurant. |
| 我今天一杯水也没喝。 | Wǒ jīntiān yì bēi shuǐ yě méi hē. | I haven't had even one glass of water today. |
| 这个菜一点儿也不辣。 | Zhège cài yìdiǎnr yě bú là. | This dish isn't spicy at all. |
| 他一点儿肉都不吃。 | Tā yìdiǎnr ròu dōu bù chī. | He doesn't eat any meat at all. |

**Watch out:** the "one" phrase goes before the verb: ✗ 我也没喝一杯水 should be 我一杯水也没喝.

### G2 · 多/少 + verb (do more / less of something)
**Pattern:** `多 / 少 + Verb (+ 一点儿 or an amount) + Object`

Put 多 or 少 directly before a verb to say that the action should happen to a greater or lesser degree than usual. It is the standard way to give advice or make requests about quantity: 多喝水, "drink more water"; 少放盐, "go easy on the salt". With a past action and a number it can also mean "too many" or "too few" compared with the correct amount, as when someone gives you the wrong change. English puts "more" after the verb, so learners are tempted to do the same in Chinese.

| Chinese | Pinyin | English |
|---|---|---|
| 天气冷了，你多穿一点儿衣服。 | Tiānqì lěng le, nǐ duō chuān yìdiǎnr yīfu. | It has turned cold. Put on a bit more clothing. |
| 师傅，请少放点儿盐。 | Shīfu, qǐng shǎo fàng diǎnr yán. | Chef, please use a little less salt. |
| 医生让我多喝水，少喝咖啡。 | Yīshēng ràng wǒ duō hē shuǐ, shǎo hē kāfēi. | The doctor told me to drink more water and less coffee. |
| 服务员多找了我五块钱。 | Fúwùyuán duō zhǎo le wǒ wǔ kuài qián. | The waiter gave me five kuai too much change. |

**Watch out:** 多 and 少 come before the verb, not after it: ✗ 你吃多青菜 should be 你多吃青菜.

### G3 · 刚 (just) compared with 刚才 (just now)
**Pattern:** `Subject + 刚 + Verb` compared with `刚才 (+ Subject) + Verb … (+ 了)`

Both words point to the recent past, but they are different kinds of word. 刚 is an adverb: it sits right before the verb and says the action was completed only a short while ago, from the speaker's point of view. The sentence normally has no final 了, and 刚 cannot be followed by 不 or 没. 刚才 is a time word like 昨天: it names the moment a few minutes ago, can go before or after the subject, accepts 了, and can be followed by a negative. The usual mistake is to treat the two as interchangeable.

| Chinese | Pinyin | English |
|---|---|---|
| 我刚吃完饭，一点儿也不饿。 | Wǒ gāng chīwán fàn, yìdiǎnr yě bú è. | I've just finished eating; I'm not hungry at all. |
| 她刚到上海，还没给我打电话。 | Tā gāng dào Shànghǎi, hái méi gěi wǒ dǎ diànhuà. | She has only just arrived in Shanghai and hasn't called me yet. |
| 你刚才去哪儿了？ | Nǐ gāngcái qù nǎr le? | Where did you go just now? |
| 刚才我很饿，现在不饿了。 | Gāngcái wǒ hěn è, xiànzài bú è le. | A moment ago I was hungry; now I'm not any more. |

**Watch out:** only 刚才 can be followed by a negative: ✗ 你刚没点菜吗 should be 你刚才没点菜吗.

### G4 · Resultative complements (I)
**Pattern:** `Verb + Result (完 / 到 / 见 / 懂 / 错 / 清楚 …) (+ Object)`; negative: `没 (有) + Verb + Result`

A Chinese verb names an action but does not say whether it got anywhere. To state the outcome, attach a second word straight after the verb: 卖完 "sell + finished" (sold out), 找到 "look for + reach" (find), 写错 "write + wrong", 听懂 "listen + understand". Verb and result form one unit, so 了 and the object follow the pair, and nothing goes between them. Because the sentence is about whether a result was achieved, the negative uses 没, not 不. Which result goes with which verb is partly a matter of habit, so learn the common pairs as ready-made words.

| Chinese | Pinyin | English |
|---|---|---|
| 对不起，小白菜今天卖完了。 | Duìbuqǐ, xiǎobáicài jīntiān màiwán le. | Sorry, the bok choy is sold out today. |
| 我找到我的饭卡了。 | Wǒ zhǎodào wǒ de fànkǎ le. | I've found my meal card. |
| 对不起，我写错了你的名字。 | Duìbuqǐ, wǒ xiěcuò le nǐ de míngzi. | Sorry, I wrote your name wrong. |
| 他说得太快，我没听清楚。 | Tā shuō de tài kuài, wǒ méi tīngqīngchu. | He spoke too fast and I didn't catch it clearly. |

**Watch out:** keep verb and result together and put 了 after both: ✗ 我找了到饭卡 should be 我找到了饭卡.

### G5 · 好 as a resultative complement
**Pattern:** `Verb + 好 (+ Object) (+ 了)`

As a result word, 好 does not mean "good". It says the action has been brought to a proper finish, so that whatever was being worked on is ready for the next step. That is a little more than 完, which only says the action is over: 做完了 means the cooking has stopped, while 做好了 means the meal is ready to eat. It is very common with 做, 准备, 点, 写 and 买. The negative is 没 + verb + 好, and learners often forget that the object comes after 好, not before it.

| Chinese | Pinyin | English |
|---|---|---|
| 饭做好了，快来吃吧。 | Fàn zuòhǎo le, kuài lái chī ba. | The food is ready. Come and eat. |
| 你们点好菜了吗？ | Nǐmen diǎnhǎo cài le ma? | Have you finished ordering? |
| 我还没准备好，请等一下。 | Wǒ hái méi zhǔnbèihǎo, qǐng děng yíxià. | I'm not ready yet. Please wait a moment. |
| 功课做好了才能看电视。 | Gōngkè zuòhǎo le cái néng kàn diànshì. | You can only watch TV once your homework is properly done. |

**Watch out:** 做得好 (does it well) describes quality, while 做好了 (has got it done) reports a result; do not mix them up.

### G6 · Adjective reduplication (I)
**Pattern:** `AA + 的 (+ Noun)` (one-syllable adjective, said twice)

Saying a one-syllable adjective twice and adding 的 makes a description more vivid and warmer in tone, a bit like "nice and sweet" or "lovely and tall" in English. The doubled form can describe a noun directly (小小的饭馆) or act as the predicate (汤酸酸的). It paints a picture, so it is used for things you can see, taste or feel, not for cool objective statements. A doubled adjective already carries its own degree, so it cannot take 很, 非常 or 不. Adding 很 is the mistake to avoid.

| Chinese | Pinyin | English |
|---|---|---|
| 这个汤酸酸的、辣辣的，我很喜欢。 | Zhège tāng suānsuān de, làlà de, wǒ hěn xǐhuan. | This soup is nice and sour and spicy. I really like it. |
| 糖醋鱼甜甜的，孩子都喜欢吃。 | Tángcùyú tiántián de, háizi dōu xǐhuan chī. | Sweet-and-sour fish is lovely and sweet, and kids all like it. |
| 那个小小的饭馆，菜很好吃。 | Nàge xiǎoxiǎo de fànguǎn, cài hěn hǎochī. | That tiny little restaurant has delicious food. |
| 他弟弟高高的，很帅。 | Tā dìdi gāogāo de, hěn shuài. | His younger brother is nice and tall, and very handsome. |

**Watch out:** no degree adverb before a doubled adjective: ✗ 很甜甜的 should be 甜甜的 or 很甜.

### G7 · The verb 来 used when ordering
**Pattern:** `(再 +) 来 + Number + Measure word + Dish or drink`

In restaurants, canteens and at food stalls, 来 stands in for "bring us" or "we'll have". It is casual and friendly, and it saves you from choosing a more specific verb. Always follow it with a quantity: a number, a measure word and then the item. 再来 asks for one more of something, and 先来 says what you want to start with. The verb here has nothing to do with anyone "coming", which confuses learners at first, and the usage is limited to ordering food, drink and similar goods.

| Chinese | Pinyin | English |
|---|---|---|
| 先来两碗酸辣汤吧。 | Xiān lái liǎng wǎn suānlàtāng ba. | Let's start with two bowls of hot and sour soup. |
| 师傅，来一个红烧牛肉，少放点儿盐。 | Shīfu, lái yí ge hóngshāo niúròu, shǎo fàng diǎnr yán. | Chef, one braised beef, please, and go light on the salt. |
| 再来三碗米饭，谢谢。 | Zài lái sān wǎn mǐfàn, xièxie. | Three more bowls of rice, thanks. |
| 我们来一盘素饺子，怎么样？ | Wǒmen lái yì pán sù jiǎozi, zěnmeyàng? | How about we get a plate of vegetarian dumplings? |

**Watch out:** do not drop the quantity: ✗ 来饺子 should be 来一盘饺子.

## Dialogues

### Dialogue 1 · Nothing spicy, please
*Setting: On Friday evening 林月 takes 马克 to a small, crowded home-style restaurant near campus.*

| Speaker | Chinese | Pinyin | English |
|---|---|---|---|
| 林月 | 这个饭馆的家常菜很好，可是人真多，好像没有位子了。 | Zhège fànguǎn de jiācháng cài hěn hǎo, kěshì rén zhēn duō, hǎoxiàng méiyǒu wèizi le. | This restaurant's home-style dishes are great, but it's so crowded. It looks like there are no seats left. |
| 服务员 | 两位吗？那张桌子刚准备好，请坐。 | Liǎng wèi ma? Nà zhāng zhuōzi gāng zhǔnbèihǎo, qǐng zuò. | Two of you? That table has just been made ready. Please sit down. |
| 马克 | 太好了！我很饿，我们快点菜吧。 | Tài hǎo le! Wǒ hěn è, wǒmen kuài diǎncài ba. | Great! I'm really hungry. Let's order quickly. |
| 林月 | 服务员，来一盘素饺子，一个家常豆腐，豆腐做辣一点儿。 | Fúwùyuán, lái yì pán sù jiǎozi, yí ge jiācháng dòufu, dòufu zuò là yìdiǎnr. | Waiter, a plate of vegetarian dumplings and a home-style tofu, and make the tofu a bit spicier. |
| 马克 | 等一下！我一点儿辣的都不能吃。 | Děng yíxià! Wǒ yìdiǎnr là de dōu bù néng chī. | Hang on! I can't eat anything spicy at all. |
| 林月 | 好吧，豆腐不要辣的。那我自己来一碗酸辣汤。 | Hǎo ba, dòufu bú yào là de. Nà wǒ zìjǐ lái yì wǎn suānlàtāng. | Fine, no spice in the tofu. Then I'll have a bowl of hot and sour soup for myself. |
| 服务员 | 好。对不起，素饺子刚卖完，你们要不要点些青菜？ | Hǎo. Duìbuqǐ, sù jiǎozi gāng màiwán, nǐmen yào bu yào diǎn xiē qīngcài? | OK. Sorry, the vegetarian dumplings have just sold out. Would you like to order some greens? |
| 马克 | 饺子没有了？那来一盘小白菜吧，少放点儿盐。 | Jiǎozi méiyǒu le? Nà lái yì pán xiǎobáicài ba, shǎo fàng diǎnr yán. | No dumplings left? Then a plate of bok choy, please, with not too much salt. |
| 服务员 | 没问题，这儿上菜很快。你们喝什么？ | Méi wèntí, zhèr shàngcài hěn kuài. Nǐmen hē shénme? | No problem, the food comes out fast here. What would you like to drink? |
| 林月 | 我很渴，要一杯冰茶。马克，你呢？ | Wǒ hěn kě, yào yì bēi bīngchá. Mǎkè, nǐ ne? | I'm really thirsty. An iced tea for me. Mark, how about you? |
| 马克 | 我也要冰茶，多放点儿冰。这些菜够吗？ | Wǒ yě yào bīngchá, duō fàng diǎnr bīng. Zhèxiē cài gòu ma? | Iced tea for me too, with extra ice. Is this enough food? |
| 林月 | 够了，不够再点。 | Gòu le, bú gòu zài diǎn. | It's enough. If it isn't, we'll order more. |

## Exercises

### E1 · Choose the right word {type=choice}
1. 我今天很忙，一杯水___没喝。 [也 | 就 | 才] → 也
2. 天气冷了，你___穿点儿衣服。 [多 | 很 | 更] → 多
3. ___你去哪儿了？我们都在找你。 [刚才 | 刚 | 又] → 刚才

5. 服务员，再___两碗米饭。 [来 | 去 | 走] → 来

### E2 · Put the words in order {type=order}
1. 都 / 位子 / 饭馆里 / 没有 / 一个 → 饭馆里一个位子都没有。
2. 少 / 请 / 盐 / 放 / 一点儿 → 请少放一点儿盐。
3. 吃完 / 刚 / 我 / 午饭 → 我刚吃完午饭。
4. 没 / 清楚 / 我 / 听 / 对不起 → 对不起，我没听清楚。
5. 一盘 / 来 / 我们 / 饺子 / 先 → 我们先来一盘饺子。

### E3 · Translate into Chinese {type=translate}

4. I forgot to bring my meal card. → 我忘了带饭卡。
5. Waiter, one more bowl of hot and sour soup. → 服务员，再来一碗酸辣汤。
