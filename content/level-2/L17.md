# Lesson 17 · 租房子 · Renting an Apartment

## Objectives
- After this lesson you can describe a home: its rooms, its furniture, and whether it is quiet, clean or noisy.
- After this lesson you can say how long something has been going on up to now, or how much of it you have done so far.
- After this lesson you can stress a point with 连…都/也… and say that something does not fit with Verb + 不下.
- After this lesson you can give rough amounts with 多 (over 800 dollars, a bit more than an hour).
- After this lesson you can phone a landlord and ask about rent, utilities, the deposit and pets.

## Grammar

### G1 · Verb + 了 + number + measure word + noun + 了
**Pattern:** `Subject + Verb + 了 + number + measure word + noun + 了`

One 了 after the verb simply reports a finished amount: 我看了三套公寓 means the viewing is over and the total was three. Add a second 了 at the very end of the sentence and the meaning becomes "so far": the count or the length of time has reached this point and may well keep growing. Use it for running totals (cups of coffee today, ads you have read) and for things still going on (how long you have lived somewhere). The commonest mistake is leaving out the final 了, which quietly turns "I have lived here for two years" into "I lived here for two years".

| Chinese | Pinyin | English |
|---|---|---|
| 我看了三套公寓了，还想再看两套。 | Wǒ kànle sān tào gōngyù le, hái xiǎng zài kàn liǎng tào. | I have looked at three apartments so far and still want to see two more. |
| 她在这儿住了两年了。 | Tā zài zhèr zhùle liǎng nián le. | She has been living here for two years now. |
| 我们已经付了四个月的房租了。 | Wǒmen yǐjīng fùle sì ge yuè de fángzū le. | We have already paid four months of rent so far. |
| 他今天喝了五杯咖啡了。 | Tā jīntiān hēle wǔ bēi kāfēi le. | He has had five cups of coffee so far today. |

**Watch out:** 她在这儿住了两年。 (she lived here for two years, and that is over) is not the same as 她在这儿住了两年了。 (she is still living here).

### G2 · 连…都/也… (even…)
**Pattern:** `连 + emphasised item + 都/也 + (不/没) + Verb`

连 picks out an extreme case to prove a point: if even this is true, the rest goes without saying. The emphasised item comes right after 连 and always stands before the verb, even when it is logically the object; then 都 or 也 must follow. It is most often used in negative sentences ("doesn't even have…", "didn't even…"), but positive ones work too. The usual mistakes are forgetting 都/也, or leaving the object in its normal place after the verb.

| Chinese | Pinyin | English |
|---|---|---|
| 这个房间连椅子都没有。 | Zhège fángjiān lián yǐzi dōu méiyǒu. | This room doesn't even have a chair. |
| 他忙得连饭也没吃。 | Tā máng de lián fàn yě méi chī. | He was so busy that he didn't even eat. |
| 这个字连老师都不认识。 | Zhège zì lián lǎoshī dōu bú rènshi. | Even the teacher doesn't know this character. |
| 我连房东姓什么都不知道。 | Wǒ lián fángdōng xìng shénme dōu bù zhīdào. | I don't even know the landlord's surname. |

**Watch out:** ✗ 这个房间没有连椅子。 The item goes before the verb and needs 都 or 也: 这个房间连椅子都没有。

### G3 · Potential complements (II)
**Pattern:** `Verb + 得/不 + 下`

In Lesson 16 you put 得 or 不 between a verb and its complement to say whether a result can be reached. This lesson adds the complement 下, which is about room: Verb + 不下 means there is not enough space or capacity for something, and Verb + 得下 means there is. It combines with verbs such as 放 (put), 坐 (sit), 住 (live) and 吃 (eat). The negative form is by far the more common one; the positive form turns up mostly in questions and in answers to them. Do not translate "can't fit" with 不能: 不能放 means you are not allowed to put it there, while 放不下 means it will not fit.

| Chinese | Pinyin | English |
|---|---|---|
| 卧室太小了，放不下两张床。 | Wòshì tài xiǎo le, fàng bu xià liǎng zhāng chuáng. | The bedroom is too small; two beds won't fit. |
| 这套公寓住得下四个人吗？ | Zhè tào gōngyù zhù de xià sì ge rén ma? | Can four people live in this apartment? |
| 我的车坐不下六个人。 | Wǒ de chē zuò bu xià liù ge rén. | My car can't seat six people. |
| 我吃得太多了，连蛋糕都吃不下了。 | Wǒ chī de tài duō le, lián dàngāo dōu chī bu xià le. | I have eaten too much; I can't even manage the cake. |

**Watch out:** ✗ 客厅不能放下这张桌子。 For "there isn't room", say 客厅放不下这张桌子。

### G4 · 多 for an approximate number
**Pattern:** `round number (10, 20, 100…) + 多 + measure word + noun` or `number ending in 1-9 + measure word + 多 + noun`

多 placed after a number means "and a bit more". Where it goes depends on how big the extra bit is. After a round number (ten, twenty, a hundred) the extra is some whole units, so 多 follows the number directly: 二十多个人 is twenty-something people. After a number that ends in 1 to 9 the extra is only a fraction of one unit, so 多 follows the measure word: 一个多月 is a month and a bit. The classic mistake is mixing the two orders: 十多块钱 is somewhere between eleven and nineteen yuan, but 十块多钱 is ten yuan and some change.

| Chinese | Pinyin | English |
|---|---|---|
| 房租一个月八百多美元。 | Fángzū yí ge yuè bābǎi duō měiyuán. | The rent is over eight hundred dollars a month. |
| 我等了他一个多小时。 | Wǒ děngle tā yí ge duō xiǎoshí. | I waited for him for over an hour. |
| 从公寓到学校要走二十多分钟。 | Cóng gōngyù dào xuéxiào yào zǒu èrshí duō fēnzhōng. | It takes more than twenty minutes to walk from the apartment to school. |
| 这把椅子才九块多钱。 | Zhè bǎ yǐzi cái jiǔ kuài duō qián. | This chair costs only nine yuan and a bit. |

**Watch out:** ✗ 一多个小时 is wrong; with a number from 1 to 9 the measure word comes first: 一个多小时。

### G5 · Question pronouns with 都/也
**Pattern:** `Question pronoun (+ noun) + 都/也 + (不/没) + Verb`

Followed by 都 or 也, a question word stops asking and starts covering every possibility: 谁都 is "everybody", 什么都 "everything", 哪儿都 "everywhere", 什么时候都 "any time". With 不 or 没 the meaning flips to "nobody", "nothing", "nowhere". 都 is fine in both positive and negative sentences, while 也 is used almost only in negative ones. If the question word is the object, it has to move in front of the verb; leaving it after the verb is the mistake learners make most often.

| Chinese | Pinyin | English |
|---|---|---|
| 这套公寓什么家具都有。 | Zhè tào gōngyù shénme jiājù dōu yǒu. | This apartment has every kind of furniture. |
| 周末我哪儿也不想去。 | Zhōumò wǒ nǎr yě bù xiǎng qù. | I don't want to go anywhere this weekend. |
| 谁都喜欢安静的地方。 | Shéi dōu xǐhuan ānjìng de dìfang. | Everybody likes a quiet place. |
| 这些公寓哪套都不安静。 | Zhèxiē gōngyù nǎ tào dōu bù ānjìng. | None of these apartments is quiet. |

**Watch out:** ✗ 我不想去哪儿都。 The question word goes before 都/也 and the verb: 我哪儿都不想去。

## Exercises

### E1 · Choose the right word {type=choice}
1. 我的房间太小，___书架都放不下。 [连 | 跟 | 比] → 连
2. 她在那套公寓住了三年___，不想搬。 [了 | 过 | 的] → 了
3. 这张饭桌太大，厨房里放不___。 [下 | 懂 | 错] → 下
4. 房租一个月九百___美元。 [多 | 些 | 很] → 多
5. 这个周末我___也不想去。 [哪儿 | 什么 | 谁] → 哪儿

### E2 · Put the words in order {type=order}
1. 厨房 / 连 / 这套公寓 / 没有 / 都 → 这套公寓连厨房都没有。
2. 了 / 看了 / 我 / 广告 / 二十多个 → 我看了二十多个广告了。
3. 放不下 / 客厅 / 这套沙发 / 太小了 → 客厅太小了，放不下这套沙发。
4. 养 / 什么宠物 / 不准 / 都 / 这里 → 这里什么宠物都不准养。
5. 美元 / 这套公寓 / 八百多 / 一个月要 → 这套公寓一个月要八百多美元。

### E3 · Translate into Chinese {type=translate}
1. I have been living in this apartment for two years now. → 我在这套公寓住了两年了。
2. He doesn't even have a desk. → 他连书桌都没有。
3. This bedroom can't fit two beds. → 这个卧室放不下两张床。
4. The security deposit is a little over five hundred dollars. → 押金是五百多美元。
5. It is very quiet there; everybody likes it. → 那里很安静，谁都喜欢。

### E4 · Match the word {type=match}
1. 广告 → advertisement
2. 厨房 → kitchen
3. 卧室 → bedroom
4. 家具 → furniture
5. 干净 → clean
6. 房租 → rent
7. 押金 → security deposit
8. 宠物 → pet
